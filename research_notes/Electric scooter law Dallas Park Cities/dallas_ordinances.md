## Q1. What does the Dallas City Code itself (Chapter 28) say about where and how any motor-assisted scooter, including a private one, may be ridden?

### Takeaway
The governing section today is **Dallas City Code § 28-41.1.1, "Restrictions on the use of motor assisted scooters and electric bicycles."** § 28-41.1 is now "Reserved." The City describes § 28-41.1.1 as regulating riding and parking of scooters generally, not just rentals, and § 28-41.1.1(k) sets fines of up to $200. City-aligned 2023–2026 sources and a sitting council member say privately owned scooters may not be ridden on sidewalks, trails, parks or plazas, or on roads posted at 35 mph or more. Older (2018–2019) and some commercial sources say the sidewalk ban covers only Downtown/CBD, Deep Ellum and the Cedars. The current statutory text could not be read directly to settle this.

### Cited Findings
- **Current code structure:** The amlegal Chapter 28 table of contents lists "28-41.1. Reserved." and "Sec. 28-41.1.1. Restrictions on the use of motor assisted scooters and electric bicycles." — [Dallas City Code Ch. 28 (amlegal)](https://codelibrary.amlegal.com/codes/dallas/latest/dallas_tx/0-0-0-111903)
- **City Transportation Dept. description:** "Sec. 28.41.1.1 of the City Code contains regulations on riding and parking electric and motor-assisted scooters and bicycles." ### Inferences
- **[CLEAR]** § 28-41.1.1 is the operative Chapter 28 section for all motor-assisted scooters and e-bikes. It sits in the traffic chapter and is not limited to permit holders, and the City says it governs "riding and parking" generally. It therefore binds private-scooter riders.
- **[CLEAR]** The Council and City-aligned reporting since 2020 say there is **no sidewalk riding anywhere in Dallas**, for private as well as rented scooters. This supersedes the 2018–2019 zone-based restriction (CBD/Downtown, Deep Ellum, Cedars). Third-party guides that say sidewalks are allowed outside the CBD appear to describe the 2018–2019 regime. Rated CLEAR on direction, but the exact subsection wording was not seen.
- **[AMBIG]** Whether the **20 mph citywide cap** and the **"35 MPH or higher" road exclusion** are written into § 28-41.1.1 for all riders, or exist only in the rental Program Rules, is unresolved. The 2020 ordinance coverage and Community Impact suggest they cover everyone. The current City webpage words them as "Dockless Vehicles" rules. Note the 2022 Program Rules said "greater than 35" mph and the current page says "35 MPH or higher," a stricter test than the state's "35 mph or less" allowance.
- **[INTERP]** Read literally, Moreno's quote ("on sidewalks in public parks, plazas, and most city trails") describes park, plaza and trail sidewalks. Community Impact and BikeDFW instead list sidewalks, trails, parks and plazas as separate no-ride categories. The broader reading is more consistent with other sources.

## Q2. Which rules bind only dockless/rental operators and their users, and which bind all riders? What is the 2018–2026 timeline?

### Takeaway
Dockless rules live in **Dallas City Code Chapter 43, Article X** (originally "Dockless Vehicle Permit," Ordinance No. 30936, adopted June 27, 2018; now "Shared Dockless Vehicle Operating Permit," §§ 43-157 et seq.) and in director-issued **Shared Dockless Vehicle Program Rules** (current version posted April 17, 2026, effective April 28, 2026). These bind **permitted operators**, and through operator apps and geofencing, their customers. They include curfews (5 a.m.–9 p.m.), slow-ride zones (10 mph), geofenced no-ride zones, the $20 parking fine, deployment caps and age gates. They do not, by their terms, bind someone riding their own scooter. Private riders are bound by § 28-41.1.1 (Q1), Chapter 32 (Q4) and state law.

### Cited Findings
- **Chapter 43 Article X (Ord. 30936, header "6-26-18," adopted June 27, 2018):** § 43-157 defines a dockless vehicle as a bicycle, electric bicycle or electric motor-assisted scooter (by reference to Tex. Transp. Code §§ 541.201 and 551.351) that can be located and unlocked through a smartphone app. § 43-158 authorizes the director to set program rules. § 43-167 makes the permit nontransferable. § 43-168 covers operations (emblem, unique ID, no third-party advertising). § 43-169 covers parking and deployment: no obstructing sidewalks below 48 inches of clear width and no blocking transit stops, loading zones or driveways. — [Ordinance No. 30936 (City PDF)](https://dallascityhall.com/departments/transportation/DCH%20Documents/Dockless%20Vehicles/Dockless%20Vehicle%20Ordinance.pdf); [Dallas City Code Ch. 43 (amlegal)](https://codelibrary.amlegal.com/codes/dallas/latest/dallas_tx/0-0-0-120243)
- **Rider-education duties in Ord. 30936:** Operators must tell users that "bicyclists and motor assisted scooters must yield to pedestrians on sidewalks and trails," that "bicycles may not be ridden on sidewalks within the central business district," and that "minors must wear helmets while riding a bicycle." — [Ord. 30936](https://dallascityhall.com/departments/transportation/DCH%20Documents/Dockless%20Vehicles/Dockless%20Vehicle%20Ordinance.pdf); [Dockless Operational Guidelines (City PDF)](https://dallascityhall.com/departments/transportation/DCH%20Documents/Dockless%20Vehicles/Operational%20Guidelines.pdf)
- **Program Rules definitions:** "Director," "shared dockless vehicle" and related terms "have the meanings assigned by Section 43-157." This ties the rules to permitted shared vehicles. — [Shared Dockless Vehicle Program Rules (Apr. 2024 version)](https://dallascityhall.com/departments/transportation/DCH%20Documents/Dockless%20Vehicles/042024_DVProgram%20Rules.pdf)
- **2020 suspension:** Rules required companies to shut down after 9 p.m. in Deep Ellum and after midnight elsewhere, and businesses reported non-compliance. The City halted the program effective **September 2, 2020**, citing public safety. — [KERA, Aug. 31, 2020](https://www.keranews.org/government/2020-08-31/city-of-dallas-halts-scooter-program-following-public-safety-concerns); [Dallas Morning News, Aug. 31, 2020](https://www.dallasnews.com/news/2020/08/31/electric-scooters-must-be-taken-off-dallas-streets-by-the-end-of-the-week-city-says/)

### Inferences
- **[CLEAR]** Rental or operator only (not binding on private owners by their own terms): the permit and fees, deployment caps, the **5 a.m.–9 p.m. curfew** and FIFA pilot, **geofenced slow-ride zones** (Bishop Arts, Deep Ellum, Farmers Market, Victory Park, Ronald Kirk Bridge), the $20 improper-parking fine and account suspension, the 16+ age gate, equity zones and § 43-169 parking and deployment duties. All of these sit in Chapter 43 Article X or Program Rules issued under § 43-158, and both are keyed to "shared dockless vehicle" (§ 43-157).
- **[CLEAR]** Binding on all riders, including private owners: § 28-41.1.1 (sidewalks, riding and parking, up to $200 fine), Chapter 32 park rules, and state law.
- **[AMBIG]** City-aligned sources (Community Impact 2023, Council member Moreno, BikeDFW) say the **no-ride categories** (sidewalks, trails, parks, plazas, roads of 35+ mph) apply to private scooters too. The City webpage itself frames them as "Dockless Vehicles" rules. They apply to private riders only to the extent § 28-41.1.1 or Chapter 32 independently prohibits them. No source shows that geofenced slow-zone speeds (10 mph) bind private riders.
- **[INTERP]** The 2026 memo's "state fair grounds" (Fair Park) and "public plazas" exclusions are Program Rules. Any equivalent private-rider ban would need a code basis, for example Chapter 32 if the area is parkland, or § 28-41.1.1.

## Q3. What is the status of Dallas helmet rules (bicycles, scooters, minors)?

### Takeaway
Dallas City Code **Chapter 9, Article II (§§ 9-7 to 9-8)** requires bicycle helmets for minors and makes parents liable. The adult requirement was dropped in 2014, with a June 2014 Council briefing showing it under review. Whether a Dallas helmet requirement extends to **motor-assisted scooters** is **ambiguous**. 2020 coverage says minors must wear helmets on motorized scooters, while 2023 coverage says helmets are not required.

### Cited Findings
- **§ 9-8 text (Parks-hosted copy):** "(b) A parent or guardian of a minor commits an offense if he knowingly causes or permits, or by insufficient control allows, the minor to operate or ride upon a bicycle without a helmet." That copy still shows a subsection (a) for riders, and § 9-7 defines "minor" as a person "under 17 years of age." — [Dallas City Code Chapter 9, Article II, Bicycle Helmets (dallasparks.org)](https://www.dallasparks.org/DocumentCenter/View/745/Dallas-City-Code-Chapter-9-Bicycle-Helmets)
- **2014 review:** A June 4, 2014 Council briefing listed the "all age groups" bicycle helmet requirement (Sec. 9-8) for review, with options to delete it or limit it to minors. — [City of Dallas "Bicycle Use Ordinance" briefing, June 4, 2014](https://dallascityhall.com/government/Council%20Meeting%20Documents/2014/BicycleUseOrdinance_060414.pdf); [Dallas Observer commentary, 2014](https://www.dallasobserver.com/news/the-helmet-rule-isnt-dallas-only-stupid-bike-law-its-also-illegal-to-do-a-wheelie-7144155/)
- **Current status (secondary):** A law-firm summary says § 9-8 requires helmets for anyone under 18. Secondary sources report the Council partially repealed the ordinance in June 2014, so it now applies only to riders under 18. — [O'Hare Koch, "Rules of the Road: 6 Dallas Bike Laws"](https://www.oharelawfirm.com/dallas-personal-injury-attorney/rules-of-the-road-6-dallas-bike-laws-you-should-know/); [NCTCOG legislation resources](https://www.nctcog.org/trans/plan/lumo/bikeped/resources/legislation)
- **Scooters:** The March 2020 rules reportedly said "Minors must wear helmets while riding motorized scooters." — [Dallas Morning News, Mar. 26, 2020](https://www.dallasnews.com/news/public-health/2020/03/26/at-an-uncertain-time-for-motorized-scooters-dallas-city-council-approves-stricter-rules-heftier-fees-for-companies/). Earlier, the Observer said riders 17 and under must wear a helmet. — [Dallas Observer](https://www.dallasobserver.com/news/dallas-police-have-issued-about-20-citations-to-people-riding-scooters-illegally-11727533/). In 2023: "Helmets aren't required to drive a scooter around Dallas." — [Dallas Morning News, May 23, 2023](https://www.dallasnews.com/news/2023/05/23/e-scooters-are-making-a-comeback-in-dallas-what-rules-should-riders-keep-in-mind/)

### Inferences
- **[CLEAR]** No Dallas helmet requirement for **adult** bicyclists or adult scooter riders was found.
- **[AMBIG]** The age cutoff is either under 17 (the Parks-hosted copy, which may be pre-2014 text) or under 18 (2014-and-later secondary sources). Also ambiguous is whether § 28-41.1.1 contains a minor-helmet clause for scooters. The 2020 reporting and the parental-offense language in Ord. 31048 suggest something like it, but the 2023 DMN says no helmet is required.

## Q4. What do Dallas parks and trails rules say (Chapter 32, Park Board, Katy Trail, Klyde Warren, other trails)?

### Takeaway
Dallas has excluded scooters from city trails since 2018 (Ord. 31048 barred them from bicycle-only paths and trails). 2023–2026 City and Council statements treat **all public parks, trails and plazas** as no-ride zones for private as well as rented scooters, with a Program Rules exception for the Ronald Kirk Pedestrian Bridge. The Katy Trail's official rules say "No motorized vehicles permitted." Klyde Warren Park, run by a private foundation, prohibits riding scooters through the park. I did **not** find a Park and Recreation Board resolution on e-scooters or e-bikes or a posted trail speed limit. Chapter 32's vehicle sections (§§ 32-8, 32-81) were not retrievable.

## Q5. What are the penalties?

### Takeaway
§ 28-41.1.1(k) sets a fine of up to **$200** for riders who violate Dallas scooter and e-bike rules. This applies to private riders, and the City and media have consistently said so since 2018. The rental program adds a separate **$20** improper-parking fine and account suspensions, which are operator/program sanctions. I could not verify the Dallas general penalty clause (§ 1-5) or a municipal-court fine schedule.

### Cited Findings
- "Fines of up to $200 (Section 28-41.1.1(k) of the Dallas City Code)." — [City of Dallas program page](https://dallascityhall.com/departments/transportation/Pages/dockless-vehicle-program.aspx); [BikeDFW](https://www.bikedfw.org/bike--scooter-share.html)
- Tickets "punishable by a fine of up to $200." — [CBS Texas, "Dallas Police Issuing Tickets To Scooter Riders Breaking Law"](https://www.cbsnews.com/amp/texas/news/dallas-police-issuing-tickets-to-scooter-riders-breaking-law); [Fox 4](https://www.fox4news.com/news/electric-scooters-banned-from-dallas-parks-trails)

### Takeaway
