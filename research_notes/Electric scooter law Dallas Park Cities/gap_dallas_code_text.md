## Q1. Exact wording of § 28-41.1.1 (a)–(k), its definitions, and whether its restrictions bind ALL riders (including privately owned scooters) or only dockless/rental vehicles

### Takeaway
The verbatim current text of § 28-41.1.1 could not be retrieved. The City of Dallas's own program page, Community Impact, CBS Texas and a council member all say the no-ride rules apply to **privately owned and rented scooters alike**. Those rules cover sidewalks, trails, parks, plazas and roads posted 35 mph or higher, with fines up to $200 under § 28-41.1.1(k). The city also describes the 20 mph citywide cap as a general rule. The 9 p.m. curfew and the 10 mph slow-ride zones look like rental-program rules. The helmet rule applies only to minors, and the age cutoff conflicts between sources.

### Cited Findings
**Section title and penalty subsection**
- The section heading appears in search snippets of the amlegal code as "Sec. 28-41.1.1. Restrictions on the use of motor assisted scooters and electric bicycles." (snippet). The same snippet showed a speed-regulation subheading, but the page text came back jumbled — [amlegal Dallas Ch. 28](https://codelibrary.amlegal.com/codes/dallas/latest/dallas_tx/0-0-0-111903)
- The City's Dockless Vehicle Program page says § 28.41.1.1 of the City Code holds the rules on riding and parking electric and motor-assisted scooters and bicycles. Riders who break those rules can be fined **up to $200 under Section 28-41.1.1(k)** (paraphrase of the city page) — [Dallas Dockless Vehicle Program page](https://dallascityhall.com/departments/transportation/Pages/dockless-vehicle-program.aspx)

**Applicability to privately owned scooters (location bans)**
- City text as quoted on the Bike DFW page: "Scooters (rented and privately owned) may not be ridden on or in: sidewalks, trails, parks, plazas, on roads with a speed limit of 35 MPH or higher, and in other designated No-Ride Zones." (snippet) — [Bike DFW, Bike & Scooter Share](https://www.bikedfw.org/bike--scooter-share.html). The search tool also attributed this "rented and privately owned" wording to the City's Shared Dockless Vehicle Program page — [Dallas Dockless Vehicle Program page](https://dallascityhall.com/departments/transportation/Pages/dockless-vehicle-program.aspx)
- **Wording conflict:** another search summary paraphrased the same city page as "**dockless vehicles** may not be ridden on or in sidewalks, trails, parks, plazas, on roads with a speed limit of 35 MPH or higher" — [Dallas Dockless Vehicle Program page](https://dallascityhall.com/departments/transportation/Pages/dockless-vehicle-program.aspx). I could not tell whether the page says "scooters (rented and privately owned)" or "dockless vehicles", or whether its wording changed between versions.

- CBS Texas (2023 relaunch): a council member said it is illegal to ride "rented, or privately owned scooters" on sidewalks, in public parks, in plazas and on "most city trails" (snippet and paraphrase) — [CBS Texas, rental scooters back](https://www.cbsnews.com/amp/texas/news/rental-scooters-e-bikes-officially-back-on-dallas-streets)
- An earlier search summary also reported that the 35 mph road ban applies "whether rented from the city or privately owned" — [Community Impact, 2023-04-12](https://communityimpact.com/dallas-fort-worth/lake-highlands-lakewood/transportation/2023/04/12/dallas-implements-new-rules-for-electric-scooter-riders-providers-in-program-relaunch/)

**Sidewalk-ban details (conflicting)**

- Bike DFW and a 2026 vendor guide both describe an exception allowing sidewalk riding "when you're starting or ending your ride" (paraphrase; I could not confirm the code text) — [Bike DFW](https://www.bikedfw.org/bike--scooter-share.html)

That matches the **2019** regime and appears outdated — [Reyes Law](https://www.reyeslaw.com/blog/can-you-ride-an-e-scooter-on-the-sidewalk-in-texas/); [Dallas News Curious Texas, 2019-06-07](https://www.dallasnews.com/news/curious-texas/2019/06/07/where-can-you-ride-a-motorized-scooter-in-dallas-curious-texas-investigates/)

**Speed**

**Definitions and "person" framing (2018 text; current text unverified)**
- Ord. 31048 (2018) defines a motor assisted scooter as a self-propelled device with at least two wheels in contact with the ground, a braking system, a gas or electric motor of no more than 40 cc, and a deck for standing or sitting. The definition excludes mopeds, motorcycles, electric bicycles and motorized mobility devices (paraphrase) — [Ord. 31048 PDF](https://files.amlegal.com/pdffiles/Dallas/31048.pdf)
- Ord. 31048 frames the offense as general: a person "commits an offense if he operates or rides a motor assisted scooter on any city-owned property" (snippet; this is the § 28-41.1 fallback provision) — [Ord. 31048 PDF](https://files.amlegal.com/pdffiles/Dallas/31048.pdf)
- The 2022 amendment was described as providing "safety, parking, and speed limit requirements for motor assisted scooters and electric bicycles, and a penalty not to exceed $200," and as retitling the section to cover electric bicycles (paraphrase of a search summary) — [amlegal PDF 32235](https://files.amlegal.com/pdffiles/Dallas/32235.pdf)

### Inferences
- **Sidewalk, trail, park, plaza and 35+ mph road bans apply to private riders: clearly established as the City's own stated position.** The basis is the "rented and privately owned" wording on the City page, two news outlets, and a council member's statement. Without the verbatim code text, this rests on the City's description rather than on the ordinance itself. The conflicting "dockless vehicles" paraphrase of the same page keeps a small residual doubt.
- **The 20 mph citywide cap applies to all riders: ambiguous, leaning yes.** The City presents it as "city-wide" for scooters and e-bikes and does not limit it to rentals. The amlegal snippet shows a speed subheading inside § 28-41.1.1, which suggests it is in the code rather than only in the program rules.
- **10 mph slow-ride zones bind private riders: subject to interpretation.** Coverage describes them as geofenced zones on rental scooters. I found no source saying they bind private riders.
- **The 9 p.m. curfew is a rental-operator/program rule, not a general rider rule: ambiguous, leaning rental-only.** Staff could change it administratively without council approval in 2024, which points to the program rules. No source says it binds private riders.
- **Helmets: required only for minors; adults not required. The minor-helmet rule itself is clearly established, but the age threshold (under 17 vs. under 18) is ambiguous.** KERA (2023) says under 17. The 2018 briefing (a proposal) said under 18.
- **Sidewalk "start/end ride" exception: ambiguous.** It appears only in secondary sources.
- The 2018 offenses were framed as "a person commits an offense", not as obligations on operators or dockless vehicles. If the 2022 text kept that drafting style, the rider restrictions would reach anyone riding, private riders included. That inference is consistent with the City's statements but unverified.

### Gaps
- **Verbatim text of § 28-41.1.1(a)–(k): unresolved.** The amlegal code, the amlegal ordinance PDFs (31048, 32235) and the city PDFs could not be fetched, and search snippets never reproduced the subsections. I cannot say which lettered subsection holds the sidewalk, 35 mph road, trail/park/plaza, speed, helmet or parking rules, except that (k) is the penalty.
- **Current statutory definitions of "motor assisted scooter", "electric bicycle", "dockless vehicle", "operator" and "No-Ride Zone" in § 28-41.1.1: unresolved.**
- Whether § 28-41.1 (the older city-property ban that takes effect if § 28-41.1.1 sunsets) still exists after the 2020 and 2022 amendments: unresolved.
- Whether the $20 improper-parking fine KERA mentions is a code penalty or a vendor/program fee: unresolved — [KERA](https://www.keranews.org/transportation/2023-06-02/dallas-scooters-are-back-whats-changed)

## Q3. Chapter 32 (Parks) provisions on vehicles/motorized devices, Park Department rules on e-scooters, and coverage of named trails

### Takeaway
The Park and Recreation Department states plainly that "Motorized scooters are not permitted in City of Dallas parks or on trails." That rule and the § 28-41.1.1 no-ride rules on trails, parks and plazas, which carry the $200 fine, appear to be the operative bans. Chapter 32 has older general provisions: § 32-2, "Speed limit; driving upon walks", and § 32-8, "Driving and parking of vehicles". I could not retrieve their verbatim current text, and I found no Park Board rule specific to e-scooters.

### Cited Findings
- Dallas Parks trail etiquette page: "Motorized scooters are not permitted in City of Dallas parks or on trails." (snippet) — [Dallas Parks, Etiquette & Emergency System](https://www.dallasparks.org/150/Etiquette-Emergency-System)
- A Dallas Parks–hosted copy of Chapter 32 shows **§ 32-2, "Speed limit; driving upon walks"** (derived from Ord. 8019). It makes it unlawful to ride or drive faster than posted limits on park drives or streets, "or to ride any wheel on a walk in such park" (snippet; partial). It also prohibits climbing trees, fences or buildings — [Dallas City Code Ch. 32 (dallasparks.org copy)](https://dallasparks.org/DocumentCenter/View/743/Dallas-City-Code---Chapter-32?bidId=); [Dallas Parks Ch. 32 page](https://www.dallasparks.org/116/Dallas-City-Code---Chapter-32)
- The same scraped copy contains motor-vehicle language: a person who knowingly drives or parks a **motor vehicle** in a park, including blocking trail entrances, commits an offense. Defenses cover a roadway, trail or parking area set up for public motor vehicle use, temporary mechanical failure, an order from a police or park officer, and city employees on duty. This probably belongs to **§ 32-8, "Driving and parking of vehicles"**, not § 32-2. The scrape did not separate the two sections cleanly (paraphrase) — [Dallas City Code Ch. 32 (dallasparks.org copy)](https://www.dallasparks.org/DocumentCenter/View/743); [eLaws mirror, Ch. 32 Art. I](http://dallas-tx.elaws.us/code/cico_apps_volii_ch32_arti)
- Legistar holds a "Chapter 32 Code Amendments" document (undated in the result; contents not retrieved) — [Legistar Ch. 32 amendments](https://cityofdallas.legistar.com/gateway.aspx?M=F&ID=0b50f05a-c237-45ab-9859-59407d154ca3.docx)
- Fox 4 reported that the scooter ban covers "all trails and city parks," including the **Katy Trail**. Officers may write tickets with a fine of up to $200 after a grace period (paraphrase; date not confirmed) — [Fox 4, scooters banned from parks/trails](https://www.fox4news.com/news/electric-scooters-banned-from-dallas-parks-trails). Parks and Recreation later put up "no motorized vehicle" signs on the Katy Trail — [Fox 4, Katy Trail signs](https://www.fox4news.com/news/new-signs-on-katy-trail-in-dallas-bans-scooters)
- Community Impact listed more than 30 "No Ride" zones in Lake Highlands/Lakewood, "including White Rock Lake, Olive Shapiro Park, Flag Pole Hill Park, Norbuck Park and Tenison Park" (snippet) — [Community Impact, 2023](https://communityimpact.com/dallas-fort-worth/lake-highlands-lakewood/transportation/2023/04/12/dallas-implements-new-rules-for-electric-scooter-riders-providers-in-program-relaunch/)
- Named no-ride examples also include Klyde Warren Park, the Katy Trail and the AT&T Discovery District plaza (paraphrase of search summary) — [Community Impact, 2023-05-23](https://communityimpact.com/dallas-fort-worth/lake-highlands-lakewood/transportation/2023/05/23/dallas-relaunching-long-awaited-electric-scooter-program-with-new-rules/)
- The Santa Fe Trail is a Dallas Parks facility, and Dallas Parks lists linear trails as park facilities — [Dallas Parks, Santa Fe Trail](https://www.dallasparks.org/facilities/facility/details/santafetrail-809); [Dallas Parks, Linear Trails](https://www.dallasparks.org/406/Linear-Trails)
- A council member described the ban as covering "most city trails" (snippet) — [CBS Texas](https://www.cbsnews.com/amp/texas/news/rental-scooters-e-bikes-officially-back-on-dallas-streets)
- The 2018 briefing mentions riding on "a public path or trail set aside for the exclusive use of bicycles". The search summary garbled whether this was permitted or prohibited (snippet) — [2018 briefing](https://dallascityhall.com/government/Council%20Meeting%20Documents/motor-assisted-scooter-ordinance-discussion_combined_060618.pdf)

### Inferences
- **Private motorized scooters are banned on Dallas park trails and in parks: clearly established as City policy and stated position.** The basis is the Parks Department page, the City no-ride list, Fox 4 and the council member. The ban is enforced at least through § 28-41.1.1 (up to $200).
- **Coverage of White Rock Lake trail and Katy Trail: clearly established.** Both are named in sources.
- **Coverage of Santa Fe Trail, SoPac Trail, Northaven Trail and the Trinity trails: subject to interpretation, leaning covered.** None is named in any source I found. They are city park or linear trails and would fall under the general "trails"/"parks" bans. The "most city trails" remark suggests some trails or segments may be excluded, possibly segments in street right-of-way or trails not owned by the city. I found no list of exceptions.
- Applying § 32-2 ("ride any wheel on a walk") or § 32-8 (motor vehicle in park) to e-scooters is **subject to interpretation**. Whether a scooter is a "motor vehicle" under Chapter 32's definitions is unverified. The scooter-specific § 28-41.1.1 and the Parks policy are the clearer authorities.

### Gaps
- Verbatim current text of §§ 32-2 and 32-8, Chapter 32 definitions (e.g., "vehicle", "motor vehicle", "trail"), and the Chapter 32 penalty section.
- Any Park Board resolution or rule specifically on e-scooters or e-bikes on trails. None found. An e-bike policy, if one exists, was not located.
- I found no source naming SoPac, Northaven, Santa Fe or the Trinity trails specifically as scooter no-ride trails, and no list of exempt trails behind "most city trails".
- The date of the Fox 4 "banned from parks, trails" article (probably 2018–2019, but unconfirmed).
- Out of scope but noticed: People Newspapers carried a Park Cities scooter item dated June 16, 2026. It was not reviewed — [People Newspapers](https://www.peoplenewspapers.com/?p=278541)

## Q4. Dallas general penalty § 1-5

### Takeaway
**Unresolved.** No search returned the text of Dallas City Code § 1-5. For scooter offenses this matters little, because § 28-41.1.1(k) sets its own fine of up to $200.

### Cited Findings
- A targeted search for Dallas "Sec. 1-5" "general penalty" returned only Dallas § 1-6, on amendments to the code and printing, and other cities' general-penalty sections. Nothing for Dallas § 1-5 — [amlegal Dallas § 1-6](https://codelibrary.amlegal.com/codes/dallas/latest/dallas_tx/0-0-0-99161)
- Scooter rider violations carry fines "up to $200" under § 28-41.1.1(k) — [Dallas Dockless Vehicle Program page](https://dallascityhall.com/departments/transportation/Pages/dockless-vehicle-program.aspx); [KERA](https://www.keranews.org/transportation/2023-06-02/dallas-scooters-are-back-whats-changed)

### Inferences
- Because § 28-41.1.1(k) specifies its own penalty (up to $200), § 1-5 most likely governs only Chapter 32 park offenses that lack their own penalty clause. That is not verified.

### Gaps
- Text and fine amounts of Dallas § 1-5, and whether Chapter 32 has its own penalty section. Do not assume the commonly cited $500/$2,000 municipal caps without checking the source.
