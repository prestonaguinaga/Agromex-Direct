# Gap-Filling: Highland Park TX Art. 12.09, University Park TX § 12.01.011, and Municipal Courts of Record (as of 2026-10-09)

Method note: WebFetch was blocked for every host I tried (ecode360.com, peoplenewspapers.com and tmcec.com all failed with DNS "ENOTFOUND"). Everything below therefore comes from web-search result snippets and search-engine summaries (25 searches), not from full page reads. "Verbatim (snippet)" means the text showed up in quotation form in a search result attributed to that page. I could not check it against the full page, so a stray word could be wrong. "Paraphrase" means the search tool summarized the page without quoting it. Results about Highland Park, Illinois (The Record North Shore, cityhpil.com), Highland Park, NJ, and University Park in other states were excluded.

## Q1. Highland Park, TX Code Article 12.09: sections, definitions, exceptions, safety finding, penalty and impound

### Takeaway
Article 12.09 is titled "Bicycles, Electric Bicycles, Motor-Assisted Scooters and Shared Mobility Devices" and runs from § 12.09.001 to § 12.09.009. Its scooter rule (§ 12.09.002, Ord. 2042, 9/10/18) is a flat ban covering any "public area" or "public right-of-way". Clearly established: the sections are 12.09.001 through 12.09.009. I found no scooter exception, no § 551.352(b) safety finding, no scooter-specific penalty, and no Article 12.09 definition of "motor-assisted scooter". That is a gap in what I could see, not proof that these provisions are absent.

### Cited Findings
- **Article title and range.** Verbatim (snippet): "ARTICLE 12.09 BICYCLES, ELECTRIC BICYCLES, MOTOR-ASSISTED SCOOTERS AND SHARED MOBILITY DEVICES (§ 12.09.001 – § 12.09.009)". Rating: clearly established. — [eCode360, HP Art. 12.09](https://ecode360.com/39247224)
- **§ 12.09.002.** Verbatim (snippet): "(Ordinance 2042 adopted 9/10/18) § 12.09.002 Motor-assisted scooters. No person may operate a motor-assisted scooter upon any public area or public right-of-way within the corporate limits of the Town." This matches what was already known. Rating: clearly established. — [eCode360, HP Art. 12.09](https://ecode360.com/39247224)
- **Definition of "shared mobility device"** (presumably in § 12.09.001). Verbatim (snippet): "Any bicycle, electric bicycle, motor-assisted scooter, or other similar personal transportation device, which is displayed, offered and/or placed for rent which may be locked and unlocked with or without the requirement of a docking station." Rating: clearly established that the text exists. Its placement in § 12.09.001 is my inference. — [eCode360, HP Art. 12.09](https://ecode360.com/39247224)
- **Definition of "public area"** (paraphrase): any outdoor area open to public use, whether the Town or a private party owns or operates it. This matches what was already known. — [eCode360, HP Art. 12.09](https://ecode360.com/39247224)
- **Definition of "public right-of-way"** (paraphrase): alleys, sidewalks, streets and similar ways that the Town owns, holds an easement over, or controls. Rating: clearly established in substance; exact wording not obtained. — [eCode360, HP Art. 12.09](https://ecode360.com/39247224)
- **Definition of "motor-assisted scooter".** The search tool said explicitly: "I did not find a definition of 'motor-assisted scooter' in the Article 12.09 excerpt." So I cannot say whether HP incorporates Transp. Code § 551.351. Rating: unresolved. — [eCode360, HP Art. 12.09](https://ecode360.com/39247224)
- **§ 12.09.003** (paraphrase with a partial quote): obeying traffic-control devices, which applies to "any person riding a bicycle or electric bicycle within the Town." It is not a scooter provision. — [eCode360, HP Art. 12.09](https://ecode360.com/39247224)
- **§ 12.09.004 Speed** (partial verbatim, snippet): "no person shall ride a bicycle or electric bicycle at a speed that is greater than is reasonable and prudent". It is not a scooter provision. — [eCode360, HP Art. 12.09](https://ecode360.com/39247224)
- **§ 12.09.007 Parking.** A secondary source quotes: "The Town of Highland Park's ordinance has prohibited dockless bicycles from being parked within the city limits. (Chapter 12, Article 12.09, Sec. 12.09.007 Parking)". Rating: ambiguous, because it is secondary and the date is unknown. — [NCTCOG Active Transportation Legislation & Resources](https://www.nctcog.org/trans/plan/lumo/bikeped/resources/legislation)
- **§§ 12.09.005, .006, .008 and .009.** No text was obtained for any of them.
- **How the scooter ban was adopted** (paraphrase of a September 2018 news item): a newly amended town ordinance bans electric scooters. The ban covers both the rental scooters that had entered the Dallas market and privately owned ones, and it was added to the ordinance governing dockless rental bikes. — [People Newspapers, "HP Bans Electric Scooters" (Sept. 2018)](https://www.peoplenewspapers.com/news/2018/09/hp-bans-electric-scooters/)
- **Impound and fees** (2018 news reports, paraphrase): bike-share companies had to collect their bikes daily or the town could impound them. Fees started at $30 for the first bike and rose to $100 for the fourth and beyond, and unclaimed bikes could be auctioned after 15 days. This was the January 2018 dockless bike-share law. Rating: ambiguous whether it is still current. The figures come from news reports, not from code text I could see. — [WFAA, "Highland Park outlaws bike sharing activity"](https://www.wfaa.com/article/news/highland-park-outlaws-bike-sharing-activity/287-505514435); [Dallas Morning News, Jan. 5, 2018](https://www.dallasnews.com/news/2018/01/05/highland-park-s-new-bike-share-law-lets-town-round-up-and-auction-off-urban-litter/)
- **Later amendment.** The ecode360 listing shows "Ordinance 2150 Amends Traffic and Vehicles Ch 12", dated 2025-09-16. Its contents are unknown: I could not tell whether it touched Article 12.09 or § 12.09.002. — [eCode360, HP Code (search listing)](https://ecode360.com/39247224)
- **E-bike rules (context, not scooters).** The Town's page says the amended ordinance on electric bicycles took effect January 1, 2025. Riders need a valid driver's license and a permit from HP DPS. Operators under 21 must wear a helmet. The bike must carry a permanent manufacturer's label showing class, top speed and motor wattage. News coverage adds that riding e-bikes on sidewalks is prohibited. — [hptx.org, E-Bike Information](https://hptx.org/1000/E---Bike-Information); [People Newspapers, Oct. 2024](https://www.peoplenewspapers.com/news/2024/10/hp-council-salutes-first-responders-regulates-e-bikes/)
- **State enabling text.** Verbatim (snippet, Transp. Code § 551.352(b)): "A county or municipality may prohibit the operation of a motor-assisted scooter on a street, highway, or sidewalk if the governing body of the county or municipality determines that the prohibition is necessary in the interest of safety." — [FindLaw, Tex. Transp. Code § 551.352](https://codes.findlaw.com/tx/transportation-code/transp-sect-551-352/)
- **General penalty.** I found nothing that adds to the known HP § 1.01.009 (up to $500). Tex. Local Gov't Code § 54.001 caps general ordinance fines at $500. — [Tex. Local Gov't Code § 54.001](https://texas.public.law/statutes/tex._local_gov't_code_section_54.001)

### Inferences
- **Reach beyond the statute (subject to interpretation).** § 12.09.002 reaches any "public area", including privately owned outdoor areas open to the public. The § 551.352(b) authority it presumably relies on covers only a "street, highway, or sidewalk", and only on a governing-body safety determination. A defendant could argue the ordinance reaches beyond the statute: for example, riding in a privately owned shopping-center lot open to the public. Whether HP relies on other home-rule or police-power authority for that reach is unknown.
- **Missing safety finding (subject to interpretation).** No § 551.352(b) safety finding appeared in the visible code text. If the finding exists, it is probably in the preamble of Ordinance 2042 rather than in the codified text, so its absence from the code is not significant on its own.
- **No exceptions seen (ambiguous).** I found no exception for mobility devices, crossings or walking a scooter. By its terms, "operate" would not cover walking an unpowered scooter.

### Gaps
- Full text of § 12.09.001, including whether "motor-assisted scooter" is defined or incorporates Transp. Code § 551.351, and of §§ 12.09.005, .006, .008 and .009. The likely subjects are shared-device permits, parking/impound and penalty, but that is unconfirmed.
- Whether any section has a scooter-specific penalty or impound rule, or exceptions for ADA/mobility devices.
- Ordinance 2042's preamble and safety finding.
- What Ordinance 2150 (9/16/2025) changed.
- The current impound fee schedule.

## Q2. University Park, TX § 12.01.011 (motor-assisted scooters): exceptions, age, speed, history

### Takeaway
§ 12.01.011 (eCode360 page "MOTOR-ASSISTED SCOOTERS", in Art. 12.01 General Provisions) bans scooters on public streets, highways and sidewalks. It carves out a "permitted area" around SMU for riders 18 and older, with a 10 mph speed limit; the SMU-area exception came in Ord. 18-048 and 18/049, adopted 12/18/18.

Ratings:
- General ban, Airline/SMU Blvd/Bush segments, 18+ and 10 mph: clearly established.
- The exact Hillcrest and Daniel segments: ambiguous, because I have only a paraphrase.

I found nothing on crossing or walk-a-scooter defenses, a penalty subsection, or post-2018 amendments.

- **§ 12.01.011(a)**, verbatim (snippet), consistent with what was already known: "Subject to the exceptions provided in this section, it shall be unlawful and an offense for any person to drive or operate a motor-assisted scooter…" Rating: clearly established. — [eCode360, UP § 12.01.011](https://ecode360.com/40048914)
- **Permitted area, items (A)–(C).** Verbatim (snippet; the search tool warned the excerpt was "garbled in places"): "(A) On the street or sidewalks on both sides of Airline Road, from SMU Boulevard to Daniel Street; (B) On the street or sidewalks on both sides of SMU Boulevard; (C) On the street or sidewalks on both sides of Bush Avenue." - **Why some segments are sidewalk-only** (paraphrase, 2018 reporting): the police chief explained that "where SMU owns property on only one side of the street, then the use of the scooter would be permitted only on the sidewalk." ### Inferences

- **Sidewalk exception is narrow (subject to interpretation).** Because some segments are sidewalk-only, riding in the street on those segments (for example on Hillcrest) would fall outside the exception. Being under 18 would make any riding in the "permitted area" an offense.

## Q3. Municipal courts of record: Highland Park, University Park and Dallas; appeal routes

### Takeaway
- **Dallas: clearly established.** Its municipal court of record has its own subchapter of Gov't Code ch. 30 (§ 30.00811 et seq.), and appeals go to the Dallas County criminal courts with criminal appellate jurisdiction (the County Criminal Courts of Appeals), on the record.
- **Highland Park and University Park: ambiguous.** I found no Gov't Code ch. 30 subchapter for either. Secondary sources describe HP as not a court of record (trial de novo). UP's code ties its court's jurisdiction to Gov't Code ch. 29, which suggests it is not a court of record, but I did not confirm this.

### Cited Findings
- **Gov't Code § 30.00811** (paraphrase): the subchapter applies to the City of Dallas. "Appellate courts" means the county criminal courts of Dallas County that have criminal appellate jurisdiction. It was renumbered from § 30.381 by Acts 1997, 75th Leg., ch. 165, and amended by Acts 1999, 76th Leg., ch. 691. Rating: clearly established. The subchapter letter was not shown in the results. — [Tex. Gov't Code § 30.00811 (public.law)](https://texas.public.law/statutes/tex._gov't_code_section_30.00811)
- **Other Dallas County cities.** Garland (§ 30.00891) and Coppell (§ 30.01441) use the same "county criminal courts of Dallas County" definition. Mesquite's bill referred to the "county courts at law of Dallas County", so the wording is not uniform. — [§ 30.00891](https://texas.public.law/statutes/tex._gov't_code_section_30.00891); [§ 30.01441](https://texas.public.law/statutes/tex._gov't_code_section_30.01441)
- **Record review in Dallas County.** A Class C conviction from a municipal court of record was appealed to "Dallas County Criminal Court of Appeals No. 1", which reviewed it on the record rather than de novo, citing Gov't Code § 30.00014(a)–(b). — [State Prosecuting Attorney, Villa PDR summary](https://www.spa.texas.gov/past-pdr-cases/villa-whitney-s)
- **Creating a court of record without a named subchapter.** Chapter 30 Subchapter A applies to each listed municipality and to any other city whose governing body creates a court of record under § 30.00003, by ordinance, when needed for more efficient case disposition. So a city can have a court of record without its own subchapter. — [Tex. Gov't Code ch. 30](https://statutes.capitol.texas.gov/Docs/GV/htm/GV.30.htm); [§ 30.00003 (public.law)](https://texas.public.law/statutes/tex._gov't_code_section_30.00003)
- **TMCEC Courts of Record list.**
  - It is self-reported: "TMCEC has not independently verified whether these are courts of record… refer to an individual municipality's charter or ordinances."
  - Dallas is on it.
  - In the visible portion, Highland Haven and Highland Village appeared but Highland Park did not. The University Park part of the list was cut off.
  - 205 courts reported court-of-record status as of May 2024.

  — [TMCEC, Courts of Record](https://www.tmcec.com/more-resources/courts_of_record/); [TMCEC Level I Overview of the Courts 2024](https://www.tmcec.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/11/Chapter-1-Overview-of-the-Courts-2024.pdf)
- **Highland Park status (secondary sources).** A 2015-era Avvo answer: "Highland Park, Texas is NOT a court of record, meaning you have the right to a trial de novo at the county court of appeals level." A traffic attorney's site calls HP a "non-court of record" with appeals to Dallas County. Rating: ambiguous; these sources are dated, self-interested and unofficial. — [Avvo](https://www.avvo.com/legal-answers/what-is-the-process-for-appealing-a-highland-park--2492460.html); [Jack Byno attorney page](https://www.jackbynoattorney.com/highland-park-traffic-ticket-attorney/)
- **University Park.** The UP code's municipal-court provision gives the court "jurisdiction as conferred by, Judicial Branch, chapter 29, Municipal Courts, Texas Gov't Code" (snippet). Code numbering conflicts between results: one listing labels Chapter 6 "Municipal Court", another shows "Chapter 7 Municipal Court (§ 7.01.001 – § 7.02.005)". I found no source that says UP's court is a court of record. Rating: ambiguous. — [eCode360, UP Municipal Court](https://ecode360.com/40048020); [eCode360, UP Ch. 6 listing](https://ecode360.com/40051654); [uptexas.org Municipal Court](https://www.uptexas.org/234/Municipal-Court)
- **General appeal rule.** Code Crim. Proc. art. 45A.202: "A de novo trial shall be held on appeal unless the appeal is taken from a municipal court of record and based on error reflected in the record." The appeal goes to the county court, or to the proper court in the county if the county court lacks jurisdiction. — [Tex. Code Crim. Proc. art. 45A.202](https://texas.public.law/statutes/tex._code_of_crim._proc._article_45a.202)
- **OCA summary.** "Most municipal courts are not courts of record"; appeals from non-record courts are tried de novo in county-level courts. — [Texas Courts: A Descriptive Summary](https://www.txcourts.gov/media/994672/Court-Overview.pdf)

### Inferences
- **Highland Park (ambiguous, leaning non-record).** An HP scooter citation would probably be appealed by trial de novo to a Dallas County county-level criminal court. Supporting this: the secondary sources, HP's apparent absence between Highland Haven and Highland Village on the TMCEC list, and the lack of any ch. 30 subchapter.
- **University Park (ambiguous).** The reference to ch. 29 (the general municipal-court chapter) rather than ch. 30 suggests a non-record court with de novo appeal. A § 30.00003 ordinance could change that, and I found none.
- **Dallas (clearly established).** Appeals are on the record to the Dallas County Criminal Courts of Appeals, with no de novo trial for errors reflected in the record.

### Gaps
- The Ch. 30 subchapter letter for Dallas. Results showed only the section number § 30.00811.
- An official confirmation (TMCEC full list, OCA list, or the city's code or charter) of HP's and UP's court-of-record status.
- Appeal-bond amounts and deadlines specific to HP and UP.
