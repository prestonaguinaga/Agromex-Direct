## 2. Highland Park, TX: scooter, micromobility, bicycle and e-bike provisions (Chapter 12, Article 12.09)

### Takeaway
Highland Park bans operation of **any** motor-assisted scooter, rental or privately owned, on **any public area or public right-of-way** in the Town. The ban came in **Ordinance 2042 (adopted Sept. 10, 2018)**. "Public area" is defined to include privately owned outdoor areas open to the public. The Town also bars parking or abandoning scooters in those places. E-bikes are allowed only under a separate permit, license and helmet regime: **Ordinance 2138 (adopted Oct. 15, 2024, effective Jan. 1, 2025)**. The code has no scooter-specific exception in the extracts seen: no crossing, ADA or age exception.

### Cited Findings
**Article title and history**
- Article 12.09 is titled "BICYCLES, ELECTRIC BICYCLES, MOTOR-ASSISTED SCOOTERS AND SHARED MOBILITY DEVICES". Its history notes cite **Ordinance 2042 adopted 9/10/18** and **"Ordinance 2138 adopted 10/15/2024"**. The article runs §§ 12.09.001–12.09.009. — [eCode360 Art. 12.09](https://ecode360.com/39247224)

**Operation ban (§ 12.09.002 per index extract)**
- "No person may operate a motor-assisted scooter upon any public area or public right-of-way within the corporate limits of the Town." — [eCode360 Art. 12.09](https://ecode360.com/39247224)
- The extracts showed **no exceptions** for crossing, ADA or mobility devices, age, or permitted zones for scooters. — [eCode360 Art. 12.09](https://ecode360.com/39247224) (extract-based; full section not seen)

**Definitions (Art. 12.09)**
- "Public area": "Any outdoor area that is open to the public for public use, whether owned or operated by the Town or a private party." — [eCode360 Art. 12.09](https://ecode360.com/39247224)
- "Public right-of-way": "Any public alley, parkway, public transportation path, roadway, alleyway, easement, sidewalk, or street that is owned, granted by easement, operated, or controlled by the Town." — [eCode360 Art. 12.09](https://ecode360.com/39247224)
- "Shared mobility device": "Any bicycle, electric bicycle, motor-assisted scooter, or other similar personal transportation device, which is displayed, offered and/or placed for rent which may be locked and unlocked with or without the requirement of a docking station." — [eCode360 Art. 12.09](https://ecode360.com/39247224); also in [General Provisions](https://ecode360.com/39246804)
- "Operator": "A corporation, firm, joint venture, limited liability company, partnership, person, or other organized entity that operates a shared mobility device, whether for profit or not for profit." — [eCode360 Art. 12.09](https://ecode360.com/39247224)
- The Chapter 12 General Provisions define "motor-assisted scooter". In the index's paraphrase, it is a self-propelled device with at least two wheels, a braking system, a motor of no more than 40 cubic centimeters, and a deck to stand or sit on, and it excludes electric personal assistive mobility devices (EPAMDs). This is not verbatim. — [Town of Highland Park: GENERAL PROVISIONS](https://ecode360.com/39246804)
- General Provisions define "sidewalk" as "the portion of a street between the curbline and the adjacent property line intended for the use of pedestrians". — [General Provisions](https://ecode360.com/39246259) (extract)

**Parking, abandonment and rental display**
- "No person shall park, or otherwise abandon, a bicycle, electric bicycle, motor-assisted scooter or shared mobility device in a public area or on a public right-of-way" [truncated]. The same extract adds "or in a manner that impedes access" to the street or right-of-way. A separate clause bars placing such devices in a public area or right-of-way to display them for rent or to carry advertising. — [eCode360 Art. 12.09](https://ecode360.com/39247224)

**Bicycles and e-bikes on sidewalks (§ 12.09.006)**
- "No person shall ride a bicycle upon the sidewalk in any commercial or business district of the Town." / "Whenever a person is riding a bicycle upon a sidewalk, such person shall yield the right-of-way to any pedestrian." / "No person shall ride an electric bicycle upon the sidewalk in any part of the Town." — [eCode360 Art. 12.09](https://ecode360.com/39247224)
- E-bikes: "E-bikes shall not be operated on any sidewalk, pedestrian walkway, jogging path, park trail, or any location normally used for pedestrian traffic and not open to motor vehicles." The section number was not shown; it is probably in § 12.09.009. — [eCode360 Art. 12.09](https://ecode360.com/39247224)
- § 12.09.009 is titled "Operation of electric bicycles (E-bikes) on public streets." It lets the Director of Public Safety revoke an e-bike permit if the holder cannot safely operate the e-bike. — [eCode360 Art. 12.09](https://ecode360.com/39247224)
- Riders on a public right-of-way have the rights and duties of a vehicle driver under state law, subject to the article's special rules. A rider who dismounts counts as a pedestrian. The article also has a speed provision, but its text was not visible. — [eCode360 Art. 12.09](https://ecode360.com/39247224)

**E-bike permit regime (official Town page)**
- The Town's "E-Bike Information" page lists these requirements: a valid driver's license, a permit from Highland Park DPS, a helmet for "Any operator under 21 years old", and a permanent manufacturer's label showing class, top speed and motor wattage. A Town-issued emblem goes on the frame. "Beginning January 2025, E-Bike Permits must be submitted in person and in writing at the Department of Public Safety lobby." — [hptx.org/1000/E---Bike-Information](https://www.hptx.org/1000/E---Bike-Information)

- D Magazine (July 28, 2026) reports that the permit requirement applies to non-residents too. The application asks for the storage street address and the operator's driver's license number. The sticker is bike-specific, and the owner must notify the Town within 10 business days of a change in ownership or storage location. Fines can reach **up to $500** in Highland Park, but the likely outcome for an unpermitted rider is a warning. — [D Magazine FrontBurner, July 2026](https://www.dmagazine.com/frontburner/2026/07/highland-park-university-park-e-bike-permits/)

**Rental and dockless history (timeline, secondary)**
- In January 2018 the Town adopted a bike-share law letting it impound and auction dockless rental bikes ("urban litter"). — [Dallas Morning News, Jan. 5, 2018](https://www.dallasnews.com/news/2018/01/05/highland-park-s-new-bike-share-law-lets-town-round-up-and-auction-off-urban-litter/); [WFAA](https://www.wfaa.com/article/news/highland-park-outlaws-bike-sharing-activity/287-505514435)
- People Newspapers ("HP Bans Electric Scooters," William Taylor, **Sept. 22, 2018**) reported that "a newly amended town ordinance" prohibits electric scooters in Highland Park. The ban was added to the dockless rental-bike ordinance and applies to rental scooters **and privately owned ones**. Council member Eric Gambrell is reported to have called scooters a danger on the sidewalk and on the street, and to have objected to operators using public property as staging areas. Only an index summary was seen, not the full article. — [People Newspapers, Sept. 22, 2018](https://www.peoplenewspapers.com/2018/09/22/hp-bans-electric-scooters/)

**Other provisions with scooter language**
- Municipal Tennis Courts: "Prohibited activities on the tennis courts include, but are not limited to, riding bicycles, the use of skates or skateboards, the use of scooters or motorized vehicles." — [eCode360, Municipal Tennis Courts](https://ecode360.com/39245750); [Use regulations; prohibited conduct](https://ecode360.com/39245756)

### Inferences
- **Clearly established:** a privately owned e-scooter, being a "motor-assisted scooter", may not be operated on any Town street, alley, sidewalk, parkway, easement, trail or other Town-controlled right-of-way. The ban is the same for rental and private scooters.
- **Clearly established on the text, subject to interpretation on enforcement:** the ban reaches private outdoor areas open to the public, because "public area" includes areas "owned or operated by … a private party". Highland Park Village's outdoor walks and parking courts are an example. Whether state authority supports regulating private property this way is subject to interpretation. The state authority speaks of "street, highway, or sidewalk" (see the state-law note below), and the Town may instead rely on general police power.
- **Ambiguous:** whether Ord. 2042 contains an express council finding that the prohibition is "necessary in the interest of safety". I did not see the ordinance text or any recital. The news coverage reports safety rationale ("danger on the sidewalk … on the street").
- **Ambiguous:** whether a stand-up e-scooter that exceeds the code's "motor-assisted scooter" definition (for example one lacking human-propulsion ability, or high-powered) falls outside Art. 12.09 and is instead treated as an unregistered motor vehicle or "motor-driven cycle" under state law. The code definition is based on the state definition. Under either reading it cannot lawfully be ridden on Town rights-of-way.
- **Subject to interpretation:** the e-bike sidewalk ban (§ 12.09.006) and any park-trail ban against the state e-bike preemption rule. D Magazine notes that Texas law, with few exceptions, says a local authority "may not prohibit the operation of an electric bicycle in an area in which the operation of a non-electric bicycle is permitted" ([D Magazine](https://www.dmagazine.com/frontburner/2026/07/highland-park-university-park-e-bike-permits/)). This does not affect the scooter ban.
- No age, helmet or equipment rule for scooters was found in Highland Park. None is needed, since operation on public ways is banned outright.

### Gaps
- I could not see the full text of §§ 12.09.001–12.09.009 or the section-specific penalty (if any) for § 12.09.002. I also could not see Ord. 2042's preamble or findings, or any impoundment provision for privately owned scooters.
- I found no Town press release or DPS page specifically about scooter enforcement, warnings or campaigns.
- **State-law note (for the report writer; another researcher may cover this):** a Texas Public Law copy of Transp. Code § 551.352 places the local-prohibition authority in subsection (b): "A county or municipality may prohibit the operation of a motor-assisted scooter on a street, highway, or sidewalk if the governing body of the county or municipality determines that the prohibition is necessary in the interest of safety." The same copy limits scooters to streets posted at 35 mph or less and allows them on bike paths and sidewalks ([texas.public.law § 551.352](https://texas.public.law/statutes/tex._transp._code_section_551.352)). The assignment referred to § 551.353. **Which numbering is current could not be verified**, because statutes.capitol.texas.gov was blocked and the copy was accessed in June 2024. The 2019 bills SB 549 and HB 4499 would have amended Subchapter E ([SB 549 engrossed](https://capitol.texas.gov/tlodocs/86R/billtext/html/SB00549E.htm)), and whether they were enacted was not confirmed.

## 3. University Park, TX: scooter, micromobility, bicycle and e-bike provisions

### Takeaway
University Park has made it "unlawful and an offense" to operate a motor-assisted scooter on any public street, highway or sidewalk since a **2009** ordinance, now codified at **§ 12.01.011**. "Street" includes alleys. The only exception is for riders **18 or older** in listed "permitted areas" bordering SMU: Airline Rd., SMU Blvd., Bush Ave., and specified Hillcrest and Daniel sidewalks. These exceptions came with the **Ordinance Nos. 18-048 / 18-049** era and the 2019 SMU pilot. **Article 12.09 ("Motor-Assisted Scooters")** regulates **rental scooter operators** (scooter-share systems: 10 mph cap, geofencing, lights). The ban and the SMU exception apply to private scooters as well. The usual citation is a **$136** municipal fine, according to SMU.

### Cited Findings
**Ban (§ 12.01.011)**
- Section heading: "§ 12.01.011 Motor-assisted scooters prohibited on street or sidewalk; exceptions." — [eCode360 Art. 12.01](https://ecode360.com/40048309)
- Subsection (a): "Subject to the exceptions provided in this section, it shall be unlawful and an offense for any person to drive or operate a motor-assisted scooter ("scooter") on a public street, highway or sidewalk in the city." — [eCode360 Art. 12.01](https://ecode360.com/40048309)
- Age-based exception: the prohibition "does not apply to a person eighteen (18) years of age or older" [truncated; in context, while operating in the permitted areas]. — [eCode360 Art. 12.01](https://ecode360.com/40048309)
- Subsection (c) lists the permitted areas (index paraphrase, not verbatim):
  - both sides of Airline Road from SMU Boulevard to Daniel
  - both sides of SMU Boulevard
  - both sides of Bush Avenue
  - the east sidewalk of Hillcrest Avenue south of Daniel Avenue
  - the south sidewalk of Daniel Avenue between Hillcrest and Airline
  - both sidewalks of Daniel Avenue from Airline Road to Durham Street

  — [eCode360 Art. 12.01](https://ecode360.com/40048309)
- The same article requires scooters to be parked upright on hard surfaces, including designated scooter parking areas, and defines "street" to include an alley. — [eCode360 Art. 12.01](https://ecode360.com/40048309)
- The penalty clause in § 12.01.011 was **not** visible in the extracts. — [eCode360 Art. 12.01](https://ecode360.com/40048309)

**Operator (rental) article: Article 12.09 "Motor-Assisted Scooters"**
- Definitions include "customer" (a renter of a shared scooter) and "scooter share system", described as short-term point-to-point rentals of scooters that can be locked and unlocked without a rack or docking station. The article applies to scooters that operators make available in the city. It requires nighttime lighting that meets the Transportation Code, scooters that "will be equipped with geofence ability", and operators to "limit the speed of scooters to not more than 10 miles per hour". It also states that scooters "may be operated only in the public right-of-way locations authorized by section 12.01.011" and that riders must obey traffic laws and keep right. Helmet offers by operators are mentioned. — [eCode360 Art. 12.09 (UP)](https://ecode360.com/40048914)

**Ordinance numbers and timeline**
- SMU's policy and guidelines state that scooter use on campus is subject to "City of University Park Ordinance No. 18-048 and Ordinance No. 18-049". — [SMU Policy 9.14](https://www.smu.edu/policy/9-police-and-public-safety/9-14-electric-scooters); [SMU Electric Scooter Guidelines and Parking](https://www.smu.edu/businessfinance/campusservices/parkingandidcardservices/transportation/parkingalternatives/electric-scooter-guidelines-and-parking)

- In February 2019, after a request from SMU and its student government, the council "agreed to make exceptions to the scooter ban as part of a trial program". Riders had to be 18 or older, speeds were limited to 10 mph, and geofencing was required. The ban "was developed in 2009 to safeguard against serious accidents and injuries". "Since it is private property, scooter use on the interior of the campus is regulated by the University and not the City." — [People Newspapers, Feb. 14, 2019](https://www.peoplenewspapers.com/2019/02/14/university-park-says-yes-to-motorized-scooters-at-smu/)

- November 2024 coverage: "Due to safety concerns, electric scooters have been banned from almost all University Park rights-of-way since 2009. The exception is certain streets and sidewalks around SMU, where riders 18 and older are permitted to operate scooters." — [People Newspapers, Nov. 25, 2024](https://www.peoplenewspapers.com/2024/11/25/up-limits-e-bike-use-to-licensed-drivers-imposes-other-restrictions/)

### Inferences
- **Clearly established:** a privately owned e-scooter may **not** be ridden on any University Park public street, alley or sidewalk, **except** by a rider 18 or older within the listed SMU-adjacent permitted areas. The rule is the same for rental and private scooters. Article 12.09 adds extra duties only for rental operators.
- **Clearly established:** riders under 18 have no lawful public-right-of-way riding area in University Park.
- **Subject to interpretation:** the exact geometry of the permitted areas, i.e. which side or sidewalk. The index list is paraphrased, and SMU refers to a map that I did not retrieve. "Daniel Street" in one extract versus "Daniel Avenue" elsewhere is probably an extract error.
- **Ambiguous:** whether the ordinance recites an express § 551.352(b)-type finding that the ban is "necessary in the interest of safety". News coverage cites "safety concerns", but I did not see the ordinance text.
- **Ambiguous:** whether Ord. 18-048 and 18-049 date from 2018 (the "18-" prefix suggests so) or were the vehicles for the February 2019 pilot amendments. They could also have been amended after the pilot expired on June 30, 2019. Since the current code still contains the permitted-area exception, it survived.

### Gaps
- I did not see the full text of § 12.01.011, including any penalty subsection and any crossing or ADA exception, or of Art. 12.09 (UP).
- I did not find the adoption dates or texts of Ord. 18-048 and 18-049, or the 2009 ordinance number.
- I did not find the full residential and business-district rule for nonelectric bicycles on sidewalks, including whether Snider Plaza counts as a "business district".

## 4. Parks, trails and special districts (Highland Park Village, Snider Plaza)

### Takeaway
Neither city's park rules name e-scooters, but each city's street and sidewalk scooter ban already covers park paths. In Highland Park the ban reaches every "public area", which includes parks and trails. Highland Park separately bans scooters on tennis courts and bans e-bikes on park trails and jogging paths. University Park's park rules ban motorized vehicles in parks, with an illustrative list that does not name scooters, and allow bicycles except where "No Bicycles" signs are posted.

### Cited Findings
**Highland Park parks**
- The Park Rules are in Chapter 1, Article 1.11, Division 2, §§ 1.11.031 to at least 1.11.046. "Park" is defined as a park, reservation or other area "owned or used by the Town, and devoted to active or passive recreation". Police may regulate activities in parks to prevent congestion. Curfew: "no person shall remain, stay or loiter in a park between the hours of 12:00 midnight and 5:00 a.m. on any day." — [Division 2: Park Rules](https://ecode360.com/39245667); [Park Rules](https://ecode360.com/39245676); [ecode360.com/39245688](https://ecode360.com/39245688)
- Lakeside Park: "No person shall picnic or lunch in the park area known as Lakeside Park, from Beverly Drive to Wycliff Avenue, on Lakeside Drive and St. Johns Drive." — [Park Rules, HP](https://ecode360.com/39245667) (extract)
- Town parks are open dawn until 10 p.m. Residents may reserve the Flippen Park Gazebo. — [hptx.org FAQ](https://www.hptx.org/m/faq?cat=32); [Parks Directory](https://hptx.org/558/Parks-Directory)
- Tennis courts explicitly prohibit "the use of scooters or motorized vehicles". — [eCode360 Municipal Tennis Courts](https://ecode360.com/39245750)

### Inferences
- **Clearly established (HP):** riding a private e-scooter in Lakeside Park, on the Turtle Creek greenbelt paths, or in Prather, Davis or Flippen parks violates § 12.09.002, because these are Town-owned or Town-used "public areas". A separate tennis-court rule also bans scooters.
- **Clearly established on the text (HP Village):** scooter operation on the Village's outdoor walks, plazas and parking courts falls within "public area". Practical enforcement on private property, and the scope of the state authority, are **subject to interpretation**. The private owner can also exclude riders under ordinary property rights.
- **Subject to interpretation (UP parks):** whether an e-scooter is a "motorized vehicle" under § 1.09.032. The list is "including" and so illustrative, and scooters are motorized, so a ban is a reasonable reading. Park paths in Goar, Curtis, Williams, Coffee, Caruth, Germany and Smith parks are also outside the § 12.01.011(c) permitted areas, and whether park paths count as "sidewalk[s]" is itself interpretive. Either way, I found no lawful basis to ride a scooter in University Park parks. Overall rating: **ambiguous as to which provision applies, but the outcome (not permitted) is well supported**.
- **Inference (Snider Plaza):** Snider Plaza is on the west side of Hillcrest, while the permitted Hillcrest segment is the **east** sidewalk south of Daniel. So Snider Plaza's streets and sidewalks appear to lie outside the permitted areas, and the general ban applies. I did not check this against the city or SMU map, so it is **subject to verification**.

## 6. SMU campus rules and legal effect

### Takeaway
SMU **allows privately owned** e-scooters on campus and **prohibits shared-use (rental)** scooters. Riders must be 18 or older and keep to 10 mph. Scooters are barred inside SMU buildings and at the George W. Bush Presidential Center. SMU describes its campus interior as private property regulated by the University. City ordinances still govern University Park public streets and sidewalks, and SMU Police may cite violations of state law and city ordinances.

### Cited Findings
- SMU Policy **9.14** "Electric Scooters and Personal Mobility Devices" allows privately owned scooters subject to local ordinances, including UP Ordinance Nos. 18-048 and 18-049. It says: "the use of shared-use Electric Scooters on the SMU campus is prohibited"; "SMU Police may issue citations to operators of Electric Scooters and Personal Mobility Devices who violate state law or City Ordinances"; and "Electric Scooters and Personal Mobility Devices are not allowed in SMU owned, leased, rented, or occupied buildings." It sets a 10 mph limit and bans tandem riding and riding under the influence. — [SMU Policy 9.14](https://www.smu.edu/policy/9-police-and-public-safety/9-14-electric-scooters)
- **Revision date conflict:** one index extract shows the policy revised **August 2023**, and another shows a revised date of **February 4, 2026**. — [SMU Policy 9.14](https://www.smu.edu/policy/9-police-and-public-safety/9-14-electric-scooters)
- The SMU Parking & ID Card Services guidelines say users must be "eighteen (18) years of age or older". They also say: "Electric scooter use may only occur in permitted areas – on designated University Park streets and sidewalks, and on the SMU campus, excluding the George W. Bush Presidential Center (see map)." And: "Electric scooters are prohibited on the rest of University Park's public streets and sidewalks. Violators may be ticketed by University Park Police and are subject to a $136 municipal fine." This page says it applies to both shared and private scooters. — [SMU Electric Scooter Guidelines and Parking](https://www.smu.edu/businessfinance/campusservices/parkingandidcardservices/transportation/parkingalternatives/electric-scooter-guidelines-and-parking)
- Hoverboards: SMU banned hoverboards and similar self-balancing devices in residential buildings effective Jan. 13, 2016 (student newspaper). — [SMU Daily Campus](https://smudailycampus.com/1032670/news/smu-bans-hoverboards/)
- On private property: "Since it is private property, scooter use on the interior of the campus is regulated by the University and not the City." — [People Newspapers, Feb. 14, 2019](https://www.peoplenewspapers.com/2019/02/14/university-park-says-yes-to-motorized-scooters-at-smu/)
- SMU's directions describe the campus area as bounded by US‑75 (Central Expressway) on the east, Hillcrest Ave. on the west, Mockingbird Ln. on the south and Daniel Ave. on the north. — [SMU Directions](https://www.smu.edu/AboutSMU/Maps/Directions)

### Inferences
- **Clearly established:** a private scooter is allowed on SMU's interior campus under SMU rules (18 or older, 10 mph, not in buildings, not at the Bush Center). Rental scooters are banned on campus under SMU policy. Riding on UP public streets and sidewalks is lawful only within the § 12.01.011(c) permitted areas.
- **Subject to interpretation:** the legal effect of breaking SMU policy on interior campus property. That would normally lead to University discipline for students and employees, or removal and a possible criminal-trespass warning for others, rather than a Transportation Code offense, unless state law is violated. SMU Police can cite state-law and ordinance violations. The trespass mechanism is not confirmed by an SMU source.
- **Ambiguous:** which campus roads are public University Park streets and which are private SMU drives. The permitted-area list (SMU Blvd., Bush Ave., Airline Rd.) suggests that at least these are public right-of-way regulated by § 12.01.011. KRLD's "SMU owns both sides" rule implies the streets themselves are city-controlled.
- **Ambiguous:** the internal conflict between SMU Policy 9.14, which bans shared scooters, and the guidelines page, which "applies to both shared and private". The likely explanation is that the guidelines predate the policy revision.

### Gaps
- I did not get the SMU campus scooter map or a street ownership list.
- I did not get the full text of Policy 9.14 or the Student Code provision.
- I did not confirm whether any SMU property lies in the City of Dallas, which would make Dallas scooter rules apply there.

## 7. Penalties and municipal courts

### Takeaway
Highland Park's general penalty (§ 1.01.009) caps fines at **$500** for ordinary offenses, with up to $2,000 for fire, zoning, health and sanitation offenses. University Park's general penalty is also § 1.01.009, but its dollar cap could not be confirmed. The commonly cited fine for a UP scooter violation is **$136** (SMU). Violations are fine-only Class C–level municipal offenses. The University Park Municipal Court is a **court of non-record**. Highland Park's court-of-record status was not found.

### Cited Findings
- HP § 1.01.009: "the violation of any such provision of this code or any such ordinance shall be punished by a fine not exceeding five hundred dollars ($500.00)." Fire safety, zoning, and public health and sanitation provisions carry up to $2,000. Offenses fined up to $500 generally require no culpable mental state. — [HP General Provisions](https://ecode360.com/39246259); [HP Code root](https://ecode360.com/39245399)
- UP § 1.01.009 is titled "General penalty for violations of code; continuing violations". The dollar amount was not visible. — [UP Art. 1.01](https://ecode360.com/40045113)
- UP scooter fine: "$136 municipal fine". — [SMU guidelines](https://www.smu.edu/businessfinance/campusservices/parkingandidcardservices/transportation/parkingalternatives/electric-scooter-guidelines-and-parking)
- HP e-bike fines up to $500; UP up to $200. — [D Magazine, July 2026](https://www.dmagazine.com/frontburner/2026/07/highland-park-university-park-e-bike-permits/)
- **University Park Municipal Court**:
  - 3800 University Blvd., University Park, TX 75205; phone 214‑987‑5331; walk-in hours Mon–Fri 8 a.m.–5 p.m.
  - Payment by mail, online or in person (cash, check, money order, cashier's check, credit card; checks payable to City of University Park).
  - "a court of non-record"; it handles Class C misdemeanors and fine-only offenses.

  — [uptexas.org/450/A-Citation](https://uptexas.org/450/A-Citation); [uptexas.org/239/Payments](https://uptexas.org/239/Payments); [uptexas.org/238/Parking-Tickets](https://uptexas.org/238/Parking-Tickets)
- **Highland Park Municipal Court**:
  - Town Hall, 4700 Drexel Drive, Highland Park, TX 75205; phone 214‑559‑9303; window Mon–Fri 7:30 a.m.–4:00 p.m.
  - Court sessions on Tuesdays.
  - 20 days from the citation to enter a plea, pay, or set a court date.
  - Mail payments by check or money order; card or cash in person and online, with a 3% card fee. In-person payment is also accepted at the HP DPS Dispatch Office.
  - Defensive-driving requests carry a "$144.00 Administrative Fee" per the FAQ extract.

  — [hptx.org/98/Municipal-Court](https://hptx.org/98/Municipal-Court); [hptx.org court FAQ](https://hptx.org/m/faq?cat=25)

### Inferences
- **Clearly established:** scooter-ban violations in both cities are fine-only municipal ordinance offenses, i.e. Class C–equivalent, heard in each city's municipal court. HP's cap is $500 unless Art. 12.09 sets its own penalty, which was not seen.
- **Ambiguous:** whether $136 is a fine-plus-costs figure or the base fine, and whether it is current, since it comes from an undated SMU page.
- Under-17 e-bike offenders in UP must appear with a parent ([People Newspapers](https://www.peoplenewspapers.com/2024/11/25/up-limits-e-bike-use-to-licensed-drivers-imposes-other-restrictions/)). That is consistent with general juvenile Class C procedure, and the same probably applies to scooter citations; that last point is an inference.

- I did not confirm the UP general penalty dollar cap.
- I did not see any scooter-specific penalty subsection in either code, or any impoundment rules for private scooters.
