# Practical E-Scooter Route Network: Dallas, Highland Park, University Park (as of 2026-10-09)

Method and reliability note (read first): All searches were run on 2026-10-09. Direct page retrieval (WebFetch and curl) was blocked by the sandbox proxy for every target domain (statutes.capitol.texas.gov, dallascityhall.com, ridedart.com, files.amlegal.com, texas.public.law, etc.), so every finding below comes from search-engine excerpts of the cited pages, not from a full read. - **[Secondary]** = news, nonprofit, Wikipedia, law-firm or retailer page.
- **[Unverified]** = anecdote, inference, or conflicting sources; field check required.

## 1. Posted speed limits on key corridors (and the statutory baseline)

### Takeaway
The legal default in all three cities is 30 mph (Texas urban-district prima facie limit; Dallas Sec. 28-43; Highland Park Sec. 12.06.002), so unlisted local streets are scooter-legal under the 35-mph rule. Dallas's codified Sec. 28-44 table shows most inner-city arterials near the Park Cities at 35 mph (Preston south of Prestondell, Hillcrest north of NW Hwy, Inwood south of Royal, Lemmon north of NW Hwy, Lovers Lane segments, Greenville south of Walnut Hill, Cedar Springs, Abrams south of Skillman, Garland Rd west of Tranquilla). The outer segments of those same corridors, plus Northwest Highway east of US 75, are 40-45 mph and off-limits except for crossing at intersections.

### Cited Findings

**Statutory baseline**
- Texas Transp. Code §551.352 (as reproduced by texas.public.law, citing the Legislature's site, accessed June 2024): a motor-assisted scooter may be operated on a street only where the posted limit is 35 mph or less. It may cross a road with a higher limit at an intersection. Subsection (d) allows operation on a bicycle path or sidewalk. A city or county may prohibit scooters on streets, highways or sidewalks if its governing body finds that a safety need exists. Except as stated, bicycle rules apply to scooters. **[Secondary reproduction of statute; official site blocked]**. Sources: [texas.public.law §551.352](https://texas.public.law/statutes/tex._transp._code_section_551.352). 2019 bills (SB 549, HB 4499) proposed rewriting the section, and some versions dropped sidewalk use. Enactment was not confirmed in the results: [SB 549 engrossed](https://capitol.texas.gov/tlodocs/86R/billtext/html/SB00549E.htm); [HB 4499 introduced](https://capitol.texas.gov/tlodocs/86R/billtext/html/HB04499I.htm). Statute text must be checked on statutes.capitol.texas.gov.
- Tex. Transp. Code §545.352(b)(1) (2024 text): 30 mph is the prima facie lawful speed in an "urban district" on a street other than an alley, and 15 mph in an alley. An "urban district" is territory with business, industry or dwelling structures spaced less than 100 ft apart for at least 1/4 mile on either side of the highway. **[Official-excerpt]** — [texas.public.law §545.352](https://texas.public.law/statutes/tex._transp._code_section_545.352); [Statutes by date, 545.352 (12/26/2024)](https://statutes.capitol.texas.gov/StatutesByDate.aspx?code=TN&value=545.352&date=12/26/2024)
- Tex. Transp. Code §545.356 lets a municipality alter prima facie limits by ordinance after an engineering and traffic investigation. Subsection (b-1) allows a lower limit of not less than 25 mph on non-state-highway streets. One excerpt says no engineering study is required if the street is in a residence district. A different excerpt frames (b-1) as applying when the prima facie limit is unreasonable or unsafe. The change takes effect when signs are erected. **[Official-excerpt; excerpts differ on conditions]** — [texas.public.law §545.356](https://texas.public.law/statutes/tex._transp._code_section_545.356); [87R HB 442 (House committee)](https://capitol.texas.gov/tlodocs/87R/billtext/pdf/HB00442H.pdf). HB 1287 (86R) proposed changing the urban default from 30 to 25 mph. Only the introduced text was found, so there is no evidence that it was enacted: [HB 1287 introduced](https://capitol.texas.gov/tlodocs/86R/billtext/html/HB01287I.htm). Proposals to allow a 20 mph floor (89R SB 2725, HB 5253) appeared, and their enactment was not confirmed: [89R SB 2725](https://capitol.texas.gov/tlodocs/89R/billtext/html/SB02725I.htm).

Dallas adopted the 2025 Dallas Bike Plan on May 28, 2025, its first update since 2011, proposing 500+ more miles in three phases. The city publishes an interactive existing-infrastructure map. ## 3. Trail network (ownership and connections)

### Takeaway
The backbone low-stress spine is Katy Trail (Victory Park/AAC to Mockingbird Station/SMU, about 3.5 mi). It connects via the Mockingbird pedestrian bridge and the SoPac/Katy extension toward White Rock Lake, and via the Northaven US 75 bridge (opened late 2023) to Northaven, Cottonwood and White Rock Creek trails. Most trails are City of Dallas park facilities, and scooter use rules must be checked separately.

### Cited Findings
- **Katy Trail:** about 3.5 mi, a 12-ft-wide path from Victory Park/American Airlines Center (southern trailhead at Victory Station, 2620 Katy Trail) to just south of Mockingbird Ln and Central Expressway at SMU/Mockingbird Station, next to the DART station. TrailLink lists 4.4 mi, probably including extensions. **[Official (Dallas County) + Secondary]** — [Dallas County Katy Trail](https://www.dallascounty.org/departments/plandev/trails/maps/katy.php); [Dallas County Katy PDF](https://www.dallascounty.org/Assets/uploads/docs/plandev/trails/14-KatyTrail.pdf); [TrailLink](https://www.traillink.com/trail/katy-trail-%28dallas%29/); [Secret Dallas](https://secretdallas.com/katy-trail-dallas/)
- Another listing gives the Katy Trail as 3.5 mi from Lyte St near AAC to Airline Rd near SMU (undated). Friends of the Katy Trail is a nonprofit that helps the City of Dallas plan, build and maintain the trail. The City owns it. **[Secondary]** — [GreenSource DFW: Friends of the Katy Trail](https://www.greensourcedfw.org/green-organizations/friends-katy-trail)
- **Mockingbird pedestrian bridge** (opened 2017) links the Katy Trail to Mockingbird Station and allows trail travel from White Rock Lake to Victory Plaza. ## 4. Municipal boundaries (how to tell where you are)

### Takeaway
Highland Park is bordered by Dallas on the south, east and west and by University Park on the north. Together they form the "Park Cities," an enclave surrounded by Dallas. HP is replacing 16 "Town Limit" signs with a dark-blue "Town of Highland Park" design. Official boundary GIS was not retrieved, so riders should use the UP interactive map or zoning map and HP's map page.

### Cited Findings
- HP is bordered on the south, east and west by Dallas and on the north by University Park. Together they form the Park Cities, an enclave of Dallas. **[Secondary]** — [Wikipedia: Highland Park, Texas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Highland_Park,_Texas)
- A 2026 People Newspapers article says the HP Town Council approved replacing 16 "Highland Park Town Limit" signs. The new design reads "Town of Highland Park" on a dark blue background, replacing signs that showed Town Hall. HP buys its signs, while UP makes its signs in-house. **[Secondary]** — [People Newspapers](https://www.peoplenewspapers.com/?p=233182); [People Newspapers](https://www.peoplenewspapers.com/?p=16803)
- Dallas considered a boundary adjustment with UP near Northwest Highway (about 18 acres around Boone Elementary and a church). State law requires adjustments to be contiguous. **[Secondary]** — [Dallas Express](https://dallasexpress.com/city/dallas-may-consider-boundary-adjustment-with-university-park/)
- UP publishes an online interactive map and a zoning map PDF that shows street names to its limits, including Lovers Ln and Boaz Ln. HP has a "Maps" page. **[Official-excerpt]** — [UP Interactive Map](https://www.uptexas.org/315/Interactive-Map); [UP Zoning Map PDF](https://www.uptexas.org/DocumentCenter/View/148/City-of-University-Park-Zoning-Map-PDF); [HP Maps](https://hptx.org/329/Maps)
- UP's truck-route table shows Preston Rd and Hillcrest Ave run their full length within UP city limits, and Lovers Ln runs from UP's west city limit to Preston, which confirms those UP segments. **[Official-excerpt]** — [UP Art. 12.06](https://ecode360.com/40048559)
- UP Police is at 3800 University Blvd. **[Official-excerpt]** — [UP Police](https://uptexas.org/government/police)

### Inferences
- At street level, boundaries are signaled by town-limit signs (HP's new dark-blue "Town of Highland Park" signs), changes in street-sign fabrication (HP buys signs while UP makes its own, so designs likely differ), and which police department patrols. None of these was confirmed with photos or specifications. **[Unverified]**
- Since HP and UP are fully surrounded by Dallas, a trip from Uptown to SMU or Preston Hollow crosses jurisdictions at least twice. Rules can change mid-route (HP Art. 12.09; Dallas CBD/Cedars/Deep Ellum sidewalk ban). **[Inference]**

### Gaps

## 5. DART and other transit integration (carrying a personal e-scooter)

### Takeaway
DART's official "Bikes on DART Rail and TRE" page explicitly mentions scooters. It says "Bikes and scooters share the space inside DART and TRE cars with persons using wheelchairs and strollers," which implies scooters are carried in the wheelchair/stroller area and must yield. No DART rule specific to folding e-scooters, bus carriage of e-scooters, or riding on platforms was found. M-Line Trolley and Dallas Streetcar policies were not found.

### Cited Findings
- DART Rail and TRE: bicycles go in the area designated for disabled customers if it is unoccupied. Bicyclists must consider how crowded the vehicle is and the effect on other passengers' safety before boarding. "Bikes and scooters share the space inside DART and TRE cars with persons using wheelchairs and strollers. Please move your bike for those persons if needed." Bicyclists may not block an occupied operator's cab. Staff may relocate or remove any bicycle that obstructs operations or passengers. **[Official-excerpt]** — [DART: Bikes on DART Rail and TRE](https://www.dart.org/riding/bikesontrains.asp); [DART guide page](https://www.dart.org/guide/supporting-services/bike-to-work/bikes-on-dart-rail-and-tre)
- Bikes can be taken aboard DART rail at all hours on all cars with no added fare. **[Secondary]** — [Transit Unlimited: DART Rail](https://transitunlimited.org/DART_Rail)
- DART buses carry bikes on front racks. A 2009 release describes heavy-duty racks on hundreds of buses, and DART also has a "Using Bike Racks on DART Buses" page. **[Official, dated]** — [DART news 2009](https://www.dart.org/news/news.asp?ID=834); [Using Bike Racks on DART Buses](https://www.dart.org/riding/bikeracksonbuses.asp)
- On buses, a two-wheeled electric device (such as a Segway) may be brought aboard only when used as a mobility device due to a disability, and it must be turned off while boarding, riding and exiting. **[Official-excerpt]** — [DART: Boarding a DART Bus/Train with a Mobility Device (PDF)](https://www.dart.org/docs/default-source/rider-assistance-program/boarding-a-dart-bus-and-train-with-a-mobility-device.pdf)
- Bicycles are not allowed on DART Paratransit vehicles. **[Official-excerpt]** — [DART Guide to Paratransit Services, March 2025](https://dartorgcmsblob.dart.org/prod/docs/default-source/paratransit-services/guide-to-paratransit-services-march-2025.pdf?sfvrsn=a4957d86_1)
- DART has a Code of Conduct page and a Service Standards document (January 2025 revisions), but their scooter or platform-riding content did not surface. **[Official, content not retrieved]** — [DART Code of Conduct](https://dart.org/riding/codeofconduct.asp); [DART Service Standards Jan 2025](https://www.dart.org/docs/default-source/board/board-documents/dartservicestandards.pdf)
- A retailer article claims peak-hour bike limits per car (6–9 AM and 3–6 PM weekdays) and scooter parking at some stations. These claims were not confirmed on DART's site. **[Secondary, commercial; Unverified]** — [Levy Electric](https://www.levyelectric.com/resources/bringing-your-bike-on-the-dart-guidelines-and-tips)
- Safety context: CBS reported an e-scooter rider colliding with a DART train at Olive St downtown. **[Secondary]** — [CBS Texas](https://www.cbsnews.com/texas/news/electric-scooter-user-collides-dart-train/)
- M-Line Trolley: a heritage streetcar in Uptown owned and operated by the McKinney Avenue Transit Authority (MATA). It has been free since 2002, funded by donations and a joint subsidy from DART and the Uptown Improvement District. No bike or scooter carriage policy was found. **[Secondary]** — [Wikipedia: M-Line Trolley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M-Line_Trolley); [Downtown Dallas: Getting around](https://downtowndallas.com/discover-downtown/getting-around/)
- Note on naming: "DART" also refers to Des Moines Area Regional Transit (ridedart.com), whose station rules ban skateboarding and bicycling at DART Central Station. Those rules do **not** apply to Dallas. Dallas DART's site is dart.org, and some search tools confuse the two. **[Official-excerpt, other agency]** — [Des Moines DART Rider Guidelines](https://www.ridedart.com/darts-rider-guidelines/)

### Inferences
- For a folding personal e-scooter on DART rail or TRE, the official text supports carrying it (powered off, not ridden) in the wheelchair/stroller bay and yielding that space. Carriage on buses is unclear, since bus racks are designed for bicycles. **[Inference from Official-excerpt; confirm with DART]**
- Riding on DART platforms is not addressed in retrieved text. Riders should assume they must walk the scooter on platforms until the Code of Conduct is checked. **[Unverified]**

### Gaps
- No DART rule specifically on folding or non-folding e-scooters, battery limits, or bus carriage was found. The DART Code of Conduct text and any DART ordinance on platform riding were not retrieved.
- MATA M-Line and Dallas Streetcar policies on carrying personal scooters were not found.

### Takeaway
