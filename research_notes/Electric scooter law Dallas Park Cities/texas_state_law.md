## Q1. Text, structure and legislative history of the motor-assisted scooter subchapter (§§ 551.351–551.353); 2023 and 2025 legislative activity

### Takeaway
Subchapter E has three sections:
- **§ 551.351** defines "motor-assisted scooter" and "pocket bike or minimotorbike".
- **§ 551.352** sets the operating rules:
  - (a) streets with a posted limit of 35 mph or less, with crossings allowed at intersections;
  - (b) counties and cities may prohibit on safety grounds;
  - (c) TxDOT may prohibit on highways on safety grounds;
  - (d) sidewalks and bicycle-only paths are allowed, and bicycle rules apply;
  - (e) motor-vehicle provisions of the title do not apply.
- **§ 551.353** makes clear the subchapter does not authorize pocket bikes.

The subchapter was created in 2005 by H.B. 1596 (79th Leg.), which reorganized 2003 provisions. Bills in 2019 (S.B. 549, H.B. 4499) and 2021 (H.B. 934) to rewrite it did not change the codified text. I found no enacted change in the 88th (2023) or 89th (2025) Legislature. Rating: text of (a), (b), (d) and (e) and the § 551.353 heading **clearly established**. "No 2023/2025 change" is **established with moderate confidence**: mirrors dated 2025 and January 2026 still show the same text, but the 2025 special sessions were not checked.

### Cited Findings
**Location and title**
- Ch. 551 is currently titled "Operation of Bicycles and Mopeds, Golf Carts, and Other Low-Powered Vehicles". The 2025 code lists "Subchapter E. Motor-Assisted Scooters" with §§ 551.351–551.353 — [Justia 2025, Ch. 551 Subch. E](https://law.justia.com/codes/texas/transportation-code/title-7/subtitle-c/chapter-551/subchapter-e/)
- The 2017 chapter title was "Operation of Bicycles, Mopeds, and Play Vehicles" — [Justia 2017 Ch. 551](https://law.justia.com/codes/texas/2017/transportation-code/title-7/subtitle-c/chapter-551/)
- The 2024 code heads § 551.351 "Definitions" (plural) — [Justia 2024 § 551.351](https://law.justia.com/codes/texas/2024/transportation-code/title-7/subtitle-c/chapter-551/subchapter-e/section-551-351/)

**§ 551.351, definition of "motor-assisted scooter" (Confirmed elements, consistent across mirrors and the TTI guide)**
- A "self-propelled device" with:
  - "at least two wheels in contact with the ground during operation";
  - "a braking system" (the tail "capable of stopping the device under typical operating conditions" was not confirmed in the extracts);
  - "a gas or electric motor not exceeding 40 cubic centimeters";
  - "a deck designed to allow a person to stand or sit while operating the device"; and
  - "the ability to be propelled by human power alone".
- It "does not include a pocket bike or a minimotorbike."
- Sources: [texas.public.law § 551.351](https://texas.public.law/statutes/tex._transp._code_section_551.351); [FindLaw § 551.351](https://codes.findlaw.com/tx/transportation-code/transp-sect-551-351/); [TTI, *An Enforcement Officer's Guide to Micromobility Devices* (2024)](https://cades.tti.tamu.edu/files/2024/06/LE_mircomobility_v2.pdf)

**§ 551.351, definition of "pocket bike or minimotorbike" (Confirmed elements, paraphrase-level)**
- A self-propelled vehicle that:
  - is equipped with an electric motor or internal combustion engine with a piston displacement of **less than 50 cubic centimeters**;
  - is designed to propel itself with **not more than two wheels** in contact with the ground;
  - has **a seat or saddle** for the operator;
  - is **not designed for use on a highway**; and
  - is **ineligible for a certificate of title under Chapter 501**.
- Sources: [texas.public.law § 551.351](https://texas.public.law/statutes/tex._transp._code_section_551.351); [Cross Timbers Gazette, "Sheriff's Corner" (Mar. 1, 2026) (secondary; cites § 551.351(2))](https://www.crosstimbersgazette.com/2026/03/01/sheriffs-corner-e-bikes-scooters-and-the-rules-that-apply/); [Rollingwood TX pocket bike page (municipal)](https://www.rollingwoodtx.gov/administration/page/pocket-bikes-and-mini-motorbike-information)

**§ 551.352, operation on roadways or sidewalks (Confirmed text fragments)**
- (a) "A motor-assisted scooter may be operated only on a street or highway for which the posted speed limit is 35 miles per hour or less." Crossing a road or street with a posted limit above 35 mph is allowed at an intersection (confirmed in substance) — [FindLaw § 551.352](https://codes.findlaw.com/tx/transportation-code/transp-sect-551-352/); [texas.public.law § 551.352](https://texas.public.law/statutes/tex._transp._code_section_551.352); [Justia 2017 Ch. 551](https://law.justia.com/codes/texas/2017/transportation-code/title-7/subtitle-c/chapter-551/)
- (b) A county or municipality may prohibit operation "on a street, highway, or sidewalk if the governing body of the county or municipality determines that the prohibition is necessary in the interest of safety" — [TTI guide (2024)](https://cades.tti.tamu.edu/files/2024/06/LE_mircomobility_v2.pdf); [Houston Ch. 45 micromobility FAQ (municipal)](https://www.houstontx.gov/parking/chapter-45-micromobility-devices-faqs.pdf)
- (c) The department "may prohibit the operation of a motor-assisted scooter on a highway if it determines that the prohibition is necessary in the interest of safety" — [Justia 2017 Ch. 551](https://law.justia.com/codes/texas/2017/transportation-code/title-7/subtitle-c/chapter-551/)
- (d) "A person may operate a motor-assisted scooter on a path set aside for the exclusive operation of bicycles or on a sidewalk." Bicycle provisions of the title apply to scooters except as otherwise provided (confirmed in substance: "Laws that apply to a bicycle also apply to a motor-assisted scooter") — [FindLaw § 551.352](https://codes.findlaw.com/tx/transportation-code/transp-sect-551-352/); [TTI guide](https://cades.tti.tamu.edu/files/2024/06/LE_mircomobility_v2.pdf)
- (e) "A provision of this title applicable to a motor vehicle does not apply to a motor-assisted scooter." The subsection letter (e) is also cited by a defense-firm blog — [FindLaw § 551.352](https://codes.findlaw.com/tx/transportation-code/transp-sect-551-352/); [Chambers Law blog (secondary)](https://chambers-law.com/austinscooter/)

### Inferences
- S.B. 549 (2019) and H.B. 934 (2021) evidently were not enacted, because the 2024–2026 mirrors still show the original 35-mph, sidewalk-permissive, no-age-limit text. **[clearly established in effect; enactment status inferred from codified text]**

## Q2. Does a typical consumer e-scooter fit § 551.351? (40 cc element, speed and wattage, human power, deck, wheels, pocket-bike exclusion)

### Takeaway
A typical stand-up e-scooter fits every element of § 551.351 on its face: two wheels, brakes, an electric motor, a standing deck, kickable, no seat. The definition has **no speed or wattage cap**, and its only motor limit is "not exceeding 40 cubic centimeters". That is a displacement measure that has no ordinary meaning for an electric hub motor. I found **no AG opinion, appellate case, or DPS/TxDOT/TxDMV guidance** on how the 40-cc limit applies to electric motors. Whether a high-power (1000 W+) or 30–40 mph scooter still qualifies is **ambiguous / subject to interpretation**. The 250–1000 W, 15–25 mph stand-up scooter with no seat is **most likely** a motor-assisted scooter.

### Cited Findings
- **Elements** (see Q1 for quotes): self-propelled device; at least two wheels in contact with the ground during operation; braking system; "gas or electric motor not exceeding 40 cubic centimeters"; deck "to allow a person to stand or sit"; "ability to be propelled by human power alone"; excludes pocket bikes and minimotorbikes — [texas.public.law § 551.351](https://texas.public.law/statutes/tex._transp._code_section_551.351); [TTI guide](https://cades.tti.tamu.edu/files/2024/06/LE_mircomobility_v2.pdf)
- **No authority on the 40-cc element.** Searches of AG opinions and case law found no opinion addressing motor-assisted scooters or the 40-cc limit for electric motors. The only AG opinion a DPS enforcement guide cites in this area is GA-0966, and it concerns neighborhood electric vehicles — [DPS Enforcement Guide for Golf Carts, NEVs and Off-Highway Vehicles (hosted by wsmaonline.org)](https://www.wsmaonline.org/Enforcement_Guide_for_Golf_Carts_NEVs_and_Off-Highway_Vehicles.pdf)
- **The Legislature has tried to fix the motor and speed specifications.** H.B. 934 (87R, 2021) would have changed the motor specifications in the definition. Per one search summary it would have "raised the power limit" and set a 30 mph road threshold, but only the introduced text was found — [H.B. 934 introduced](https://capitol.texas.gov/tlodocs/87R/billtext/html/HB00934I.htm). S.B. 549 (86R, 2019) would have imposed device speed caps of 15 mph standing and 20 mph seated — [S.B. 549 engrossed](https://capitol.texas.gov/tlodocs/86R/billtext/pdf/SB00549E.pdf). Neither change appears in the current code — [FindLaw § 551.352](https://codes.findlaw.com/tx/transportation-code/transp-sect-551-352/)
- **The pocket-bike exclusion requires a seat or saddle,** together with: less than 50 cc (electric motor or ICE), not more than two wheels on the ground, not designed for highway use, and not titleable under Ch. 501 — [texas.public.law § 551.351](https://texas.public.law/statutes/tex._transp._code_section_551.351); [Cross Timbers Gazette (secondary)](https://www.crosstimbersgazette.com/2026/03/01/sheriffs-corner-e-bikes-scooters-and-the-rules-that-apply/)
- **Pocket bikes and minimotorbikes may not use any highway, road, street, bicycle path, or sidewalk** under § 551.353 (secondary summary: operation limited to private land with the owner's consent) — [Cross Timbers Gazette (secondary)](https://www.crosstimbersgazette.com/2026/03/01/sheriffs-corner-e-bikes-scooters-and-the-rules-that-apply/); [H.B. 1596 engrossed](https://capitol.texas.gov/tlodocs/79R/billtext/html/HB01596E.htm)
- **TxDMV guidance** says that for "pocket bikes and mini scooters", "most of these vehicles do not pass a vehicle safety inspection and cannot be registered or driven on public roads" (search-extract wording) — [TxDMV, Register Your Vehicle](https://www.txdmv.gov/motorists/register-your-vehicle)

### Inferences
- **"40 cubic centimeters" applied to electric motors.** Cubic centimeters measure piston displacement, which electric motors do not have. Three readings are possible:
  - (i) The limit is satisfied, or simply inapplicable, for any electric motor, so there is effectively no power cap.
  - (ii) Courts would look for a functional equivalent, but the statute offers none (no watts or horsepower).
  - (iii) The cap modifies only "gas" motors.

  Readings (i) and (iii) lead to the same practical result: **no statutory wattage limit for electric scooters**. A prosecutor or officer could still argue that a very high-power, high-speed machine is not the low-powered "scooter" the Legislature meant, particularly given the chapter title "Other Low-Powered Vehicles". **[ambiguous]**
- **No speed cap in the definition.** A 30–40 mph stand-up scooter is not excluded by the text of § 551.351. Speed matters for *where* it may go: only roads posted 35 mph or less, under § 551.352(a). It may also matter in the bicycle-rule context, for example a prudent-speed argument via the general speed statute (not researched). **[clearly established that no speed cap is in the definition; whether a court would read one in: subject to interpretation]**
- **"Ability to be propelled by human power alone".** A kick-scooter design that can be pushed with a foot satisfies this on its face. Heavy (60+ lb) high-performance scooters are kickable in theory, so an argument that they lack the "ability" is weak but possible. **[subject to interpretation; likely satisfied]**
- **"Deck designed to allow a person to stand or sit".** A standing deck qualifies. Adding a seat does not by itself defeat the definition, because the text says "stand or sit". A seat can, however, help push a small-engine device toward the pocket-bike/minimotorbike definition (seat or saddle + under 50 cc + two wheels + not designed for highway + not titleable). **[clearly established for standing deck; seated variants ambiguous]**
- **"At least two wheels".** Two 8.5–10 inch wheels satisfy this. There is no minimum wheel size. Contrast the bicycle definition, which requires at least one wheel over 14 inches (Q3). **[clearly established]**
- **Pocket-bike exclusion for the assumed scooter.** A stand-up scooter with no seat and no saddle is **not** a pocket bike or minimotorbike, because the seat/saddle element is missing. **[clearly established]**
- **Seated, high-power scooter.** It carries the same displacement problem ("less than 50 cubic centimeters" is also undefined for electric motors) and could be classed as a pocket bike/minimotorbike. In that case it is banned from all public roads, bike paths and sidewalks under § 551.353, or it is a moped or motorcycle (Q3). **[subject to interpretation]**

### Gaps
- No AG opinion (GA- or KP-series), appellate case, or DPS/TxDOT/TxDMV interpretive document applying "40 cubic centimeters" to an electric motor was found. AG search was limited to search-engine queries; the AG's opinion database could not be accessed.
- The exact final text and outcome of H.B. 934 (87R) could not be read, including the wattage or speed figure it proposed.

## Q3. If the device is NOT a motor-assisted scooter: the § 541.201 and § 502.001 definitions and adjacent device categories

### Takeaway
If a device falls outside § 551.351, it is presumptively a **"motor vehicle"**: a "self-propelled vehicle" under § 541.201, which does not list motor-assisted scooters among its exclusions.
- With a rider's saddle and no more than three wheels, it is a **moped** (top speed 30 mph or less, no more than 5 brake horsepower) or a **motorcycle**. Either brings title, registration, license, insurance and helmet rules, and most such imports cannot be titled or registered.
- With no saddle, it is an unregistrable "motor vehicle" that is in practice not street-legal.
- It is **not** a bicycle (no pedals; wheels under 14 in), **not** an electric bicycle (no operable pedals), and **not** an EPAMD (EPAMDs have non-tandem, self-balancing wheels).

Rating: categories **clearly established**; how a given nonconforming scooter would be classified is **subject to interpretation**.

### Cited Findings
- **"Motor vehicle" (§ 541.201):** "a self-propelled vehicle or a vehicle that is propelled by electric power from overhead trolley wires." FindLaw's version adds that the term "does not include an electric bicycle or an electric personal assistive mobility device". The 2025 Justia extract did not show that exclusion; the conflict is unresolved — [FindLaw § 541.201](https://codes.findlaw.com/tx/transportation-code/transp-sect-541-201/); [Justia 2025 § 541.201](https://law.justia.com/codes/texas/transportation-code/title-7/subtitle-c/chapter-541/subchapter-c/section-541-201/)
- **"Moped" (current § 541.201(8)):** a motor vehicle with a rider's saddle and no more than three wheels on the ground. It cannot exceed 30 mph, its engine cannot produce more than five brake horsepower, and an internal combustion engine must displace 50 cc or less (paraphrase-level extract) — [FindLaw § 541.201](https://codes.findlaw.com/tx/transportation-code/transp-sect-541-201/); [Justia 2025 § 541.201](https://law.justia.com/codes/texas/transportation-code/title-7/subtitle-c/chapter-541/subchapter-c/section-541-201/)
- **Older moped definition:** "a motor-driven cycle that cannot attain a speed in one mile of more than 30 miles per hour", with a two-brake-horsepower limit — [Justia 2015 Ch. 541](https://law.justia.com/codes/texas/2015/transportation-code/title-7/subtitle-c/chapter-541/); [TMCEC 2012 chart](https://www.tmcec.com/public/files/File/Course%20Materials/FY12/Clerks/Austin/charts.pdf)
- **"Motorcycle" (current):** "a motor vehicle, other than a tractor or moped, that is equipped with a rider's saddle and designed to have when propelled not more than three wheels on the ground" — [FindLaw § 541.201](https://codes.findlaw.com/tx/transportation-code/transp-sect-541-201/); [Justia 2025 § 541.201](https://law.justia.com/codes/texas/transportation-code/title-7/subtitle-c/chapter-541/subchapter-c/section-541-201/)
- **"Motor-driven cycle":** The 2015 version read "a motorcycle equipped with a motor that has an engine piston displacement of 250 cubic centimeters or less. The term does not include an electric bicycle." Current reprints did not show the definition, so it appears to have been removed. The current § 545.065 also uses "motorcycle equipped with a motor that has an engine piston displacement of 250 cubic centimeters or less" where older text said motor-driven cycle — [Justia 2015 Ch. 541](https://law.justia.com/codes/texas/2015/transportation-code/title-7/subtitle-c/chapter-541/); [FindLaw § 545.065](https://codes.findlaw.com/tx/transportation-code/transp-sect-545-065/). S.B. 1942 (86R, 2019) proposed deleting "motor-driven cycle" from the moped definition; its enactment is unconfirmed — [S.B. 1942 introduced](https://capitol.texas.gov/tlodocs/86R/billtext/pdf/SB01942I.pdf)
- **"Bicycle":** A device that a person may ride, that is propelled by human power, and that has "two tandem wheels at least one of which is more than 14 inches in diameter". The current definition reportedly also excludes mopeds — [FindLaw § 541.201](https://codes.findlaw.com/tx/transportation-code/transp-sect-541-201/); [Justia 2025 § 541.201](https://law.justia.com/codes/texas/transportation-code/title-7/subtitle-c/chapter-541/subchapter-c/section-541-201/)
- **"Electric bicycle":** § 541.201(24) says it "has the meaning assigned by Section 664.001." Under § 664.001 an electric bicycle is a bicycle "equipped with fully operable pedals and an electric motor of fewer than 750 watts with a top assisted speed of 28 miles per hour or less". The section also defines Class 1 (pedal-assist only, 20 mph or less), Class 2 (motor can propel without pedaling, 20 mph or less), and Class 3 (pedal-assist only, more than 20 and up to 28 mph). Chapter 664 was "Added by Acts 2019, 86th Leg., R.S., Ch. 485 (H.B. 2188), Sec. 8, eff. September 1, 2019" — [Official Ch. 664 (tcss.legis.texas.gov)](https://tcss.legis.texas.gov/resources/TN/htm/TN.664.htm); [FindLaw § 664.001](https://codes.findlaw.com/tx/transportation-code/transp-sect-664-001/)
- **Electric personal assistive mobility device (EPAMD) (§ 551.201):** a single-rider device with **two non-tandem wheels** that is self-balancing and electrically propelled, with average power of 750 watts (1 hp). Under § 551.202 an EPAMD may use a street only where the limit is 30 mph or less, and otherwise only to cross directly in a crosswalk, where no sidewalk is available, or when directed. It may also use a path set aside for the exclusive operation of bicycles — [FindLaw § 551.201](https://codes.findlaw.com/tx/transportation-code/transp-sect-551-201/); [Dallas "Texas Bike Laws" compilation](https://dallascityhall.com/departments/transportation/bikeway/DCH%20Documents/pdf/Texas_Bike_Laws.pdf)
- **§ 502.001 "miniature vehicle"** was added by S.B. 1816 (89R), effective Sept. 1, 2025. It covers small four-wheel vehicles, not scooters — [TxDMV bulletin](https://content.govdelivery.com/accounts/TXDMV/bulletins/3f0420f)
- **Registration bar for certain devices.** A third-party reproduction of § 502.143 surfaced in search lists the vehicles an owner "may not register … for operation on a public highway": power sweepers, motorized mobility devices, EPAMDs, and electric bicycles. Motor-assisted scooters and pocket bikes were not listed. The TTI guide lists § 502.143 among the key micromobility statutes. This is a single secondary extract — [TTI guide](https://cades.tti.tamu.edu/files/2024/06/LE_mircomobility_v2.pdf); [thecce.org Texas Transportation Code compilation (secondary; likely source of the extract)](https://thecce.org/usa/tx/texas-transportation-code/)
- **TxDMV on "pocket bikes and mini scooters":** "most of these vehicles do not pass a vehicle safety inspection and cannot be registered or driven on public roads." A Manufacturer's Certificate of Origin from a NHTSA-registered manufacturer is required to title any motorized vehicle — [TxDMV, Register Your Vehicle](https://www.txdmv.gov/motorists/register-your-vehicle)

### Inferences
- **The assumed scooter is not a bicycle or e-bike.** It has no pedals and its 8.5–10 inch wheels are under 14 inches, so it fails both "bicycle" and "electric bicycle" (fully operable pedals). **[clearly established]**
- **Not an EPAMD.** EPAMDs have *non-tandem* self-balancing wheels (Segway-type); stand-up scooters have tandem wheels. **[clearly established]**
- **Nonconforming device with a saddle** (for example, a seated scooter treated as outside § 551.351):
  - Top speed 30 mph or less and no more than 5 brake horsepower (1000 W is about 1.34 hp): it would be a **moped**.
  - Faster: a **motorcycle**.
  - Either way it is a motor vehicle subject to title (Ch. 501), registration (Ch. 502), Class M licensing (Ch. 521), financial responsibility (Ch. 601), inspection rules and the motorcycle helmet rule (Ch. 661).
  - Without a federally compliant MCO it **cannot be titled or registered**, so in practice it is **illegal on public roads**.
  - If instead it meets the pocket-bike definition, § 551.353 keeps it off all roads, paths and sidewalks.
  - **[subject to interpretation]**
- **Nonconforming stand-up device with no saddle** (for example, an argued "not 40 cc" 60 mph scooter): it is a "self-propelled vehicle" and so a "motor vehicle", but it fits neither the motorcycle nor the moped definition (both require a rider's saddle). It would be an unregistrable motor vehicle, exposing the rider to no-registration, no-license and no-insurance charges. No case applying this was found. **[ambiguous]**
- **§ 551.352(e) matters.** The § 541.201 motor-vehicle definition does not itself exclude motor-assisted scooters, so the scooter's escape from motor-vehicle rules depends entirely on § 551.352(e) and on the device meeting § 551.351. **[clearly established]**

## Q4. Bicycle provisions applied to scooters through § 551.352(d) (§§ 551.101–551.107)

### Takeaway
Under § 551.352(d), "a provision of this title applicable to the operation of a bicycle" applies to motor-assisted scooters "except as otherwise provided". Scooter riders therefore have:
- the general rights and duties of vehicle drivers (§ 551.101);
- the passenger and seat rules (§ 551.102);
- the "as near as practicable to the right curb" rule and its exceptions (§ 551.103);
- the brake and night-lighting requirements (§ 551.104): a white front lamp visible at 500 ft and a rear red reflector or red lamp.

The scooter-specific rules in § 551.352 displace the bicycle rules where they conflict, for example on sidewalks and the 35-mph road limit. Rating: application **clearly established**; how seat-based rules (§ 551.102(a)) apply to a seatless scooter is **subject to interpretation**.

### Cited Findings
- **General application.** The TTI law-enforcement guide lists §§ 551.102–551.107 as key state laws for micromobility, and says laws that apply to a bicycle apply to a motor-assisted scooter unless otherwise provided. It describes: sidewalk use allowed, **passengers prohibited**, **lights required at night**, registration and insurance not required, and no state helmet requirement — [TTI guide (2024)](https://cades.tti.tamu.edu/files/2024/06/LE_mircomobility_v2.pdf)
- **§ 551.103 (operation on roadway).** A person operating a bicycle on a roadway who is moving slower than other traffic "shall ride as near as practicable to the right curb or edge of the roadway", unless the rider is:
  - (1) passing a vehicle moving in the same direction;
  - (2) preparing to turn left at an intersection or onto a private road or driveway;
  - (3) facing a condition on or of the roadway, such as an object, vehicle, pedestrian, animal or surface hazard, that prevents safely riding next to the right curb or edge; or
  - (4) operating in an outside lane that is (A) less than 14 feet wide with no adjacent designated bicycle lane, or (B) too narrow for a bicycle and a motor vehicle to safely travel side by side.

  (b) On a one-way roadway with two or more marked lanes, a rider may ride as near as practicable to the left curb or edge. (c) Riders may ride two abreast, subject to limits including not impeding traffic. The exceptions are confirmed in substance, and the "as near as practicable" phrase is confirmed — [Official Ch. 551 PDF](https://tcss.legis.texas.gov/resources/TN/pdf/TN.551.pdf); [FindLaw § 551.103](https://codes.findlaw.com/tx/transportation-code/transp-sect-551-103/); [LawServer § 551.103](https://www.lawserver.com/law/state/texas/tx-codes/texas_transportation_code_551-103); [Onecle § 551.103](https://law.onecle.com/texas/transportation/551.103.html)
- **§ 551.104 (safety equipment).**
  - (a) Confirmed text: "A person may not operate a bicycle unless the bicycle is equipped with a brake capable of making a braked wheel skid on dry, level, clean pavement."
  - (b) At nighttime, the bicycle needs:
    - a front lamp emitting white light visible from at least 500 feet; and
    - either a rear red reflector of a type approved by the department, visible from 50 to 300 feet, or a rear lamp emitting red light visible from 500 feet.
  - A secondary source's claim of a "25 feet from 10 mph" stopping standard is **not** in the statute.
  - Sources: [Official Ch. 551 PDF](https://tcss.legis.texas.gov/resources/TN/pdf/TN.551.pdf); [texas.public.law § 551.104](https://texas.public.law/statutes/tex._transp._code_section_551.104); [Onecle § 551.104](https://law.onecle.com/texas/transportation/551.104.html); [NCTCOG Texas bicycle law pocket guide](https://nctcog.org/getContentAsset/4371d45d-27ba-40be-87cd-d1cc1d1742a6/dfc3d011-8f63-43f6-9ed8-4b444333a1d0/Pocket_Guide.pdf)
- **Racing.** TMCEC notes that racing on a highway under § 545.420 (a motor-vehicle-oriented offense) carries up to 180 days in jail and a $2,000 fine. That shows Title 7 has offense-specific penalties that override the general one — [TMCEC Level I Traffic Law 2024–25, ch. 8](https://www.tmcec.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/11/Chapter-8-Traffic-Law.pdf)

### Inferences
- Through § 551.352(d), a scooter rider is a "vehicle operator" for most Subtitle C rules of the road: signals, signs, right-of-way and lane usage. Under § 551.101 (text unverified) the rider has the rights and duties of a vehicle driver except where Ch. 551 alters them or a duty "cannot by its nature apply". **[clearly established in principle; § 551.101 text unverified]**
- **The § 551.103 right-curb rule probably applies** to scooters on the road, with its exceptions such as narrow lanes under 14 ft and left-turn positioning. **[clearly established]**
- **§ 551.104 lighting applies at night.** That means a white front lamp visible at 500 ft and a rear red reflector or rear red lamp, plus a brake able to skid a wheel. Most consumer scooters have built-in lights; whether a small built-in tail light meets the "red reflector … approved by the department" or "visible 500 ft" standard is a factual question. **[clearly established that it applies; compliance is factual]**
- **§ 551.102 seat rule.** If it requires riding "on or astride a permanent and regular seat" (text unverified), it cannot sensibly apply to a seatless stand-up scooter, whose deck is expressly contemplated by § 551.351. The "cannot by its nature apply" clause and the "except as otherwise provided" proviso in § 551.352(d) support non-application. The **passenger** limit (no more persons than the device is designed to carry) does apply; the TTI guide says passengers are prohibited. **[subject to interpretation for the seat rule; passenger limit clearly applies]**
- **§ 551.106 municipal bicycle regulation.** If it authorizes municipalities to regulate bicycles and require registration (text unverified), that would give Dallas, Highland Park and University Park a second state-law hook, beyond § 551.352(b), to regulate scooters through bicycle rules. **[subject to interpretation]**

(d) A person operating a bicycle, coaster, sled, or toy vehicle or using roller skates may not attach either the person or the bicycle, coaster, sled, toy vehicle, or roller skates to a streetcar or vehicle on a roadway."

## Q5. Sidewalks, hike-and-bike trails, and walking the scooter

### Takeaway
State law **affirmatively permits** motor-assisted scooters on sidewalks and on paths set aside exclusively for bicycles (§ 551.352(d)). There is **no statewide sidewalk ban**. Only a county or municipality may prohibit sidewalk riding, by a safety determination under § 551.352(b). The motor-vehicle sidewalk ban in § 545.422 does not reach scooters, because of § 551.352(e) and because a scooter is not being "driven" as a motor vehicle under Title 7.
- Rating: sidewalk permission and the non-application of § 545.422 are **clearly established**.
- Whether "hike and bike trails" shared with pedestrians count as a "path set aside for the exclusive operation of bicycles" is **ambiguous**.
- Pedestrian status while walking the scooter is **subject to interpretation**.

### Cited Findings
- **§ 551.352(d):** "A person may operate a motor-assisted scooter on a path set aside for the exclusive operation of bicycles or on a sidewalk." — [FindLaw § 551.352](https://codes.findlaw.com/tx/transportation-code/transp-sect-551-352/); [TTI guide ("sidewalk use: allowed")](https://cades.tti.tamu.edu/files/2024/06/LE_mircomobility_v2.pdf)
- **§ 551.352(b):** Local governments may prohibit operation "on a street, highway, or sidewalk if the governing body … determines that the prohibition is necessary in the interest of safety." The 2005 amendment added the express sidewalk authority because the 2003 law "did not specifically address operation of these vehicles on sidewalks" — [TTI guide](https://cades.tti.tamu.edu/files/2024/06/LE_mircomobility_v2.pdf); [SRC analysis H.B. 1596](https://www.lrl.texas.gov/scanned/srcBillAnalyses/79-0/HB1596ENG.PDF)
- **§ 545.422(a):** "A person may not drive a motor vehicle on a sidewalk, sidewalk area, or hike and bike trail except on a permanent or authorized temporary driveway." (b) Subsection (a) does not prohibit operating a motor vehicle on a hike and bike trail in connection with trail maintenance. (c) "Hike and bike trail" means "a trail designed for the exclusive use of pedestrians, bicyclists, or both." — [FindLaw § 545.422](https://codes.findlaw.com/tx/transportation-code/transp-sect-545-422/); [texas.public.law § 545.422 (verified May 26, 2025)](https://texas.public.law/statutes/tex._transp._code_section_545.422)
- **§ 551.352(e):** "A provision of this title applicable to a motor vehicle does not apply to a motor-assisted scooter." — [FindLaw § 551.352](https://codes.findlaw.com/tx/transportation-code/transp-sect-551-352/)
- **Municipal pointer:** Highland Park's Code has "Article 12.09: Bicycles, Electric Bicycles, Motor-Assisted Scooters and Shared Mobility Devices" (content not reviewed; see local-law notes) — [ecode360, Town of Highland Park](https://ecode360.com/39247224)

### Inferences
- **§ 545.422 does not apply** to a conforming motor-assisted scooter. It regulates "motor vehicle[s]", and § 551.352(e) disapplies Title 7 motor-vehicle provisions, while § 551.352(d) specifically authorizes sidewalks. The specific provision controls over the general. **[clearly established]**
- **Hike-and-bike trails** are defined in § 545.422(c) as for pedestrians, bicyclists, or both. A shared-use trail is arguably not a path "set aside for the exclusive operation of bicycles", so § 551.352(d) does not expressly authorize scooter use there. But no state statute prohibits scooters on such trails either, since § 545.422 is disapplied. Trail access in practice turns on the trail owner's or city's park rules. **[ambiguous]**
- **Walking the scooter.** A person walking alongside a scooter is not "operating" it. Texas's pedestrian definition ("a person on foot", recollection, unverified) would then make the person a pedestrian subject to Ch. 552. No Texas authority on point was found. **[subject to interpretation]**
- **Sidewalk riders.** No statute found makes scooter riders yield to pedestrians on sidewalks. H.B. 934 (2021) proposed a pedestrian-yield requirement, but it was not enacted — [H.B. 934 introduced](https://capitol.texas.gov/tlodocs/87R/billtext/html/HB00934I.htm). General bicycle and vehicle duties under §§ 551.101/551.352(d), and local ordinances, may impose yielding duties. **[subject to interpretation]**

### Gaps
- The § 541.001 "pedestrian" definition and the Ch. 552 pedestrian provisions were not retrieved.
- No case or AG opinion on scooters on sidewalks or trails was found.

## Q6. Licensing, registration, title, insurance, inspection, helmet, age

### Takeaway
Under state law, a conforming motor-assisted scooter needs **no driver's license, registration, title, insurance (financial responsibility), or inspection, and there is no state helmet requirement and no statewide minimum age**. The core reason is § 551.352(e). Ch. 521 (licenses), Ch. 502 (registration), Ch. 601 (financial responsibility) and Ch. 661 (helmets) are all Title 7 provisions keyed to "motor vehicle" or "motorcycle/moped". Rating: **clearly established** for a conforming scooter. Local ordinances may add requirements such as helmets for minors (see local notes).

## Q7. DWI and other offenses; Texas case law on scooters

### Takeaway
DWI is a **Penal Code** offense (§ 49.04) keyed to "motor vehicle" as defined by **Penal Code § 32.34(a)**: "a device in, on, or by which a person or property is or may be transported or drawn on a highway, except a device used exclusively on stationary rails or tracks". That definition is broad, and Transportation Code § 551.352(e) does not control it, because (e) reaches only "this title" (Transportation Code Title 7). An e-scooter rider can therefore probably be charged with DWI. **I found no Texas appellate opinion** deciding scooter DWI or scooter classification. Rating: DWI exposure is **subject to interpretation**, though risk is high; the absence of case law is noted as a search result, not a certainty.

### Cited Findings
- **Penal Code § 49.01(3):** "Motor vehicle" has the meaning assigned by § 32.34(a). **§ 32.34(a)(2):** "a device in, on, or by which a person or property is or may be transported or drawn on a highway, except a device used exclusively on stationary rails or tracks" — [Justia 2025 Penal Code § 49.01](https://law.justia.com/codes/texas/penal-code/title-10/chapter-49/section-49-01/); [texas.public.law Penal § 49.01](https://texas.public.law/statutes/tex._penal_code_section_49.01); [FindLaw Penal § 32.34](https://codes.findlaw.com/tx/penal-code/penal-sect-32-34/)
- **Off-road vehicles:** A Texas DWI commentary surfaced in the § 49.01 search (page cites "5-9, 5-11"; exact document **not identified**) notes the definition does not require street legality, and prosecutors apply it to off-road vehicles, golf carts and go-karts. Treat as unverified secondary. The candidate pages in that result set included [Chambers Law "Common Codes"](https://chambers-law.com/common-codes/) and [Texas DWI Law Firm on § 49.01](https://www.texasdwilawfirm.com/dwi-resources/texas-penal-code-49-01/)
- **No case found.** Texas defense-firm blogs report they found **no Texas appellate decision** squarely deciding whether a motorized scooter or e-bike supports a DWI. One notes it found no cases at all charging DWI on a motorized scooter; that source is about three years old. They argue § 551.352(e) is limited to the Transportation Code (secondary; not a holding) — [Chambers Law](https://chambers-law.com/austinscooter/); [Houston DWI Lawyer blog](https://www.dwilawyerhouston.com/blog/electric-scooters-and-a-texas-dwi/); [Reyes Law](https://www.reyeslaw.com/blog/can-you-get-a-dui-on-an-e-scooter-in-texas/); [Wilson & Mills (wm-attorneys)](https://www.wm-attorneys.com/dwi-golf-cart-scooter-horse-texas/)
- **"Operating" standard:** the Court of Criminal Appeals' *Denton v. State* test asks whether, under the totality of the circumstances, the defendant "took action to affect the functioning of his vehicle in a manner that would enable the vehicle's use." This was cited via a secondary source; the case was not pulled — [search set: Texas DWI blogs above](https://www.dwilawyerhouston.com/blog/electric-scooters-and-a-texas-dwi/)

- **DWI.** The § 32.34 definition does not even require a motor; it requires only a device transporting a person on a highway. A powered scooter on a street fits comfortably. - **Traffic offenses.** By § 551.352(d), the scooter rider must obey the Subtitle C rules a bicyclist must obey: signals, signs, right-of-way, the right-curb rule, and lights. Through the general misdemeanor clause, violations are fine-only misdemeanors (Q9). **[clearly established]**
- **Other Penal Code offenses keyed to the § 32.34 "motor vehicle" definition** (for example intoxication assault or manslaughter, § 49.07/49.08) would likely apply on the same reasoning. **[subject to interpretation]**

## Q8. State highways, TxDOT, frontage roads and controlled-access highways

### Takeaway
Three independent state-law limits keep scooters off high-speed state roads:
1. **§ 551.352(a)** confines scooters to streets posted 35 mph or less, with intersection crossings allowed. This excludes virtually all freeways and most frontage roads.
2. **§ 551.352(c)** lets TxDOT ("the department") prohibit scooters on a highway in the interest of safety.
3. **§ 545.065** lets the Transportation Commission, and local authorities for their roads, prohibit pedestrians, bicycles, electric bicycles, small motorcycles and "nonmotorized traffic" on limited-access or controlled-access highways, with signs required.

I found **no 43 TAC rule or Commission minute order** specifically on motor-assisted scooters. Rating: the 35-mph limit and the TxDOT authority are **clearly established**; whether § 545.065 reaches scooters directly is **ambiguous**, and in practice moot because of the 35-mph limit.

### Cited Findings
- **§ 551.352(a):** "may be operated only on a street or highway for which the posted speed limit is 35 miles per hour or less", with crossing allowed at intersections — [FindLaw § 551.352](https://codes.findlaw.com/tx/transportation-code/transp-sect-551-352/)
- **§ 551.352(c):** the department "may prohibit the operation of a motor-assisted scooter on a highway if it determines that the prohibition is necessary in the interest of safety" — [Justia 2017 Ch. 551](https://law.justia.com/codes/texas/2017/transportation-code/title-7/subtitle-c/chapter-551/)
- **§ 545.065(a):** The Commission, by recorded resolution or order, may prohibit use of a limited-access or controlled-access state highway by "a parade, funeral procession, pedestrian, bicycle, electric bicycle, motorcycle equipped with a motor that has an engine piston displacement of 250 cubic centimeters or less, or nonmotorized traffic". (b) "If the commission adopts a rule under Subsection (a), the commission shall erect and maintain official traffic-control devices on the portions of the limited-access or controlled-access highway to which the rule applies." (c)–(d) give the same power and signing duty to a local authority for its roadways. Some older mirrors list "motor-driven cycle" in place of the 250-cc motorcycle phrase — [FindLaw § 545.065](https://codes.findlaw.com/tx/transportation-code/transp-sect-545-065/); [texas.public.law § 545.065](https://texas.public.law/statutes/tex._transp._code_section_545.065); [Official Ch. 545 PDF](https://tcss.legis.texas.gov/resources/TN/pdf/TN.545.pdf)
- **TxDOT bicycle FAQ** restates the Commission's § 545.065 authority. It also says a cyclist need not use an adjacent off-road path and may use the roadway — [TxDOT Laws and Regulations FAQ (bicycle)](https://www.txdot.gov/inside-txdot/modes-of-travel/bicycle/know/laws.html)
- **H.B. 1596 (2005)** fixed a 2003 conflict in which two bills gave scooter highway-regulation authority to different state agencies — [House Committee Report H.B. 1596](https://capitol.texas.gov/tlodocs/79R/analysis/html/HB01596H.htm)

### Inferences
- **Freeways and controlled-access main lanes** (posted above 35 mph) are off-limits to scooters by § 551.352(a) regardless of signs. **[clearly established]**
- **Frontage roads.** Most Dallas-area frontage roads are posted at 40–45 mph or more, which excludes scooters. A frontage segment posted 35 mph or less would be lawful unless TxDOT or the city prohibits it. Posted limits are factual and location-specific. **[clearly established rule; application factual]**
- **§ 545.065 does not list "motor-assisted scooter".** A scooter could be reached as "nonmotorized traffic" (doubtful, since it has a motor) or through § 551.352(d)'s import of bicycle provisions (arguable). It is moot in practice because of the 35-mph rule. **[ambiguous]**
- **Crossings.** Crossing a road posted above 35 mph at an intersection is expressly allowed; mid-block crossing of such a road is not. **[clearly established]**

### Gaps
- I could not check 43 TAC (Texas Administrative Code, TxDOT rules), Transportation Commission minute orders, or TxDOT district actions for any scooter-specific prohibition under § 551.352(c). None were found in search.
- I did not check whether specific state highways in Dallas, Highland Park or University Park carry posted speeds of 35 mph or less (for example Preston Rd., Mockingbird Ln., Hillcrest Ave., and US-75/I-75 frontage roads). That may be covered by the routes researcher.

## Q9. State-law penalties

### Takeaway
A violation of § 551.352 (or of an imported bicycle rule) is an offense under § 542.301 ("an act prohibited or … an act required by this subtitle"). Unless otherwise provided it is a **misdemeanor punishable by a fine of $1 to $200** (§ 542.401), with no jail. That makes it a fine-only misdemeanor, equivalent in severity to a Class C, but § 542.401 sets its own range rather than invoking Penal Code § 12.23 ($500 max). Offense-specific penalties override. Local ordinance violations under § 551.352(b) carry municipal penalties (not covered here). Rating: **clearly established**.

### Cited Findings
- **§ 542.301:** a person commits an offense if they perform an act prohibited, or fail to perform an act required, by this subtitle. "Except as otherwise provided, an offense under this subtitle is a misdemeanor." FindLaw shows "last updated January 01, 2026" — [FindLaw § 542.301](https://codes.findlaw.com/tx/transportation-code/transp-sect-542-301/); [texas.public.law § 542.301](https://texas.public.law/statutes/tex._transp._code_section_542.301)
- **§ 542.401:** a person convicted of a misdemeanor under the subtitle "for which another penalty is not provided shall be punished by a fine of not less than $1 or more than $200" — [texas.public.law § 542.401](https://texas.public.law/statutes/tex._transp._code_section_542.401); [TMCEC Level I 2024–25 Traffic Law](https://www.tmcec.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/11/Chapter-8-Traffic-Law.pdf)
- **TMCEC on how the penalties fit together:**
  - "General penalty provisions only govern fine ranges when a more specific fine is not included in the statute."
  - "Traffic offenses function akin to strict liability offenses" (no culpable mental state generally required).
  - Source: [TMCEC Level I 2024–25 Traffic Law](https://www.tmcec.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/11/Chapter-8-Traffic-Law.pdf)
- **Racing on a highway (§ 545.420)** carries up to 180 days in jail and a $2,000 fine, an example of an offense-specific penalty — [TMCEC (via search extract)](https://www.tmcec.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/11/Chapter-8-Traffic-Law.pdf)

### Inferences
- **Covered conduct.** Ch. 551 is in Subtitle C (Rules of the Road), so §§ 542.301/542.401 apply. Examples:
  - riding on a road posted above 35 mph (§ 551.352(a));
  - riding at night without a lamp or reflector (§ 551.104 via § 551.352(d));
  - violating the right-curb rule (§ 551.103).

  Each is a $1–$200 fine-only misdemeanor unless a specific penalty applies. **[clearly established]**
- **Court costs** in Texas fine-only cases add substantially to the fine, but they were not researched. **[gap]**

### Gaps
- Whether any Ch. 551 section carries its own penalty was not verified.
- Municipal-ordinance penalty ranges were not researched (Local Government Code § 54.001 general cap of $500, or $2,000 for certain categories, is from background knowledge and unverified here).

## Q10. Official agency guidance and AG opinions; rental vs. private

### Takeaway
- **Agency guidance.** I found no TxDOT, DPS or TxDMV FAQ addressing "motor-assisted scooters" by name.
  - TxDMV's registration page warns that most "pocket bikes and mini scooters" cannot pass inspection or be registered for public roads.
  - The most useful quasi-official guidance is the **2024 Texas A&M Transportation Institute (TTI) Enforcement Officer's Guide to Micromobility Devices**.
- **AG opinions.** I found no Texas Attorney General opinion addressing motor-assisted scooters.
  - **GA-0966** is cited by DPS for neighborhood electric vehicles.
  - **KP-0331** (Sept. 14, 2020) surfaced in searches but its subject was not confirmed.
- **Rental vs. private.** State law does not distinguish them; dockless and shared-scooter regulation is local.

Rating: "no AG opinion or appellate case on point" is **established with moderate confidence** (limited search).

### Cited Findings
