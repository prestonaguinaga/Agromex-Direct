## 1. Governing procedure: Code of Criminal Procedure Chapter 45A (formerly Chapter 45) — complaint, appearance/FTA, pleas, jury, counsel, pretrial, deferral, DSC, indigency, prosecutor, trial, judgment

### Takeaway
Since **January 1, 2025**, cases in justice and municipal courts are governed by **Code Crim. Proc. Chapter 45A**. H.B. 4504 (88th Leg., R.S., 2023, Acts ch. 765) enacted it as a non-substantive recodification and repealed former Chapter 45 (Rating: **clearly established**). These Chapter 45A article numbers are confirmed:
- 45A.001 (purpose)
- 45A.003 (applicability; other Code provisions fill gaps)
- 45A.004 (Rules of Evidence apply)
- 45A.005 (prosecution)
- 45A.101 (complaint requisites; former 45.019)
- 45A.155 (jury waiver; former 45.025)
- 45A.201 (new trial)
- 45A.202 (appeal)
- 45A.203 (appeal bond)
- 45A.251 (judgment; former 45.041)
- 45A.252 (ability-to-pay inquiry)
- 45A.254 (community service; former 45.049)
- 45A.257 (waiver of fine/costs; former 45.0491)
- 45A.302–.303 (deferred disposition; former 45.051)
- Subchapter H, 45A.351 et seq. (driving safety course dismissal; former 45.0511)

Driving-safety-course dismissal requires an offense that "involves the operation of a motor vehicle." It therefore very likely does **not** apply to an e-scooter offense (Rating: **subject to interpretation**; see the inference below). Deferred disposition (45A.302) does apply to any fine-only misdemeanor (Rating: **clearly established**).

**General provisions**
- [V] **45A.001**: the chapter sets procedures for these courts. Its aims are to give fair notice and an opportunity to be heard, to keep proceedings dignified without excess formality, and to avoid unnecessary expense or delay — [Justia, 45A.001](https://law.justia.com/codes/texas/code-of-criminal-procedure/title-1/chapter-45a/subchapter-a/article-45a-001/)
- [V] **45A.003 (Applicability)**: proceedings must follow this chapter. Where the chapter has no rule, the judge applies the Code's other general provisions as needed. This is the hook that brings art. 39.14 discovery, ch. 24 subpoenas, art. 28.01 pretrial and arts. 27.08/27.09 exceptions into municipal court (see Inferences) — [Justia, Subch. A](https://law.justia.com/codes/texas/code-of-criminal-procedure/title-1/chapter-45a/subchapter-a/)
- [V] **45A.004**: the rules of evidence that apply in district court also apply in justice and municipal courts — [Justia, Subch. A](https://law.justia.com/codes/texas/code-of-criminal-procedure/title-1/chapter-45a/subchapter-a/)
- [V] **45A.005 (Prosecution)**: with the county attorney's consent, a municipal attorney may prosecute an appeal from municipal court — [Justia, Subch. A](https://law.justia.com/codes/texas/code-of-criminal-procedure/title-1/chapter-45a/subchapter-a/)

**Complaint requirements — art. 45A.101 (former 45.019)**
- [V] A complaint is sufficient, "without regard to form," if it **substantially** satisfies the listed requisites:
  - it is in writing
  - it names the accused (or reasonably describes them)
  - it alleges the offense, or that the affiant has good reason to believe and does believe the accused committed it
  - it states the offense date as definitely as the affiant can
  - it bears the affiant's signature or mark
  - it "(7) concludes with the words 'Against the peace and dignity of the State.'"
  - "If the offense charged is an offense under a municipal ordinance only, the complaint may also conclude with the words 'Contrary to the said ordinance.'"
  - In municipal court it must allege the offense was committed "in the territorial limits of the municipality in which the complaint is made"
  - Source: [FindLaw, art. 45A.101](https://codes.findlaw.com/tx/code-of-criminal-procedure/crim-ptx-crim-pro-art-45a-101/)
- [V] TMCEC: "All municipal court complaints, including complaints for city ordinance violations, must begin with the words, 'In the name and by the authority of the State of Texas.'" A complaint need only meet the requisites; it does not have to track the Code verbatim. The affiant does not need personal knowledge of the facts. (TMCEC's 2024–25 guides still cite former 45.019.) — [TMCEC Level I 2024–25, ch. 4 "Charging and Pre-Trial"](https://www.tmcec.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/11/Chapter-4-Charging-and-Pre-trial-2024.pdf); [TMCEC Level II 2024–25, ch. 2 "Municipal Court Processes"](https://www.tmcec.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/Chapter-2-Municipal-Court-Processes-2024-1.pdf)

**Jury trial**
- [V] **Art. 45A.155 (Jury Waiver), verbatim per the search excerpt:** "(a) A defendant may waive a trial by jury in writing. (b) If a defendant waives a trial by jury, the justice or judge shall hear and determine the case without a jury." — [Justia 2024, 45A.155](https://law.justia.com/codes/texas/2024/code-of-criminal-procedure/title-1/chapter-45a/subchapter-d/article-45a-155/); [FindLaw, 45A.155](https://codes.findlaw.com/tx/code-of-criminal-procedure/crim-ptx-crim-pro-art-45a-155/)
- [V] Under former art. 45.027 the municipal-court jury is **six** qualified persons selected from the venire (TMCEC 2022–23 Level I manual). The 45A successor article was not confirmed — [TMCEC Level I 2022–23, ch. 5](https://www.tmcec.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/07/08-Level-I-Ch-5.pdf)

**Deferred disposition — arts. 45A.302–.303 (former 45.051)**
- [V] **45A.302** lets the judge, "on a plea of guilty or nolo contendere" or "on a finding of guilt," in a misdemeanor "punishable by fine only," and with "payment of all court costs," defer further proceedings **without entering an adjudication of guilt** for up to **180 days**.
- [V] The judge may impose a **"special expense fee"** not to exceed the amount of the fine that could be imposed. Former 45.051 described this as a "fine." The fee is credited against any fine later imposed and may be waived for good cause.
- [V] Under 45A.302(e), a deferral order ends liability on any bond posted for the charge.
- Sources: [Justia 2025, 45A.302](https://law.justia.com/codes/texas/code-of-criminal-procedure/title-1/chapter-45a/subchapter-g/article-45a-302/); [texas.public.law, 45A.302](https://texas.public.law/statutes/tex._code_of_crim._proc._article_45a.302)
- [V] **45A.303** lists the conditions a judge may impose during deferral. They include completing a driving safety course approved under Education Code ch. 1001 "or another course as directed by the judge," and the article allows installment payment of the related fee — [FindLaw, 45A.303](https://codes.findlaw.com/tx/code-of-criminal-procedure/crim-ptx-crim-pro-art-45a-303/); [texas.public.law, 45A.303](https://texas.public.law/statutes/tex._code_of_crim._proc._article_45a.303)
- [V] TMCEC secondary materials on deferral: [TMCEC "Deferred Proceedings" (2021)](https://www.tmcec.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/12/09-Deferred-Proceedings-FINAL.pdf). Example local request forms: [Grand Prairie](https://www.gptx.org/Departments/Municipal-Court/Dismissals-Deferred-Disposition); [Melissa instruction sheet](https://www.cityofmelissa.com/DocumentCenter/View/1154/Deferred-Disposition---Instructions-and-Request-Form)

**Driving safety course (DSC) — Subch. H, 45A.351 et seq. (former 45.0511)**
- [V] Former art. 45.0511(a) limited DSC dismissal to an alleged offense that (1) is within the jurisdiction of a justice or municipal court, (2) **"involves the operation of a motor vehicle,"** and (3) is a listed moving-type offense (Transp. Code Subtitle C, etc.). The TMCEC Bench Book restates this three-part test. The search did **not** retrieve the 45A.351+ text, so the carry-over of the "motor vehicle" element is presumed (non-substantive recodification) but **unverified** — [FindLaw, former 45.0511](https://codes.findlaw.com/tx/code-of-criminal-procedure/crim-ptx-crim-pro-art-45-0511/); [TMCEC Bench Book Dec. 2023, ch. 5 "Driving Safety Courses"](https://www.tmcec.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/12/Chapter-5-Driving-Safety-Courses.pdf); [Justia Subch. H](https://law.justia.com/codes/texas/2024/code-of-criminal-procedure/title-1/chapter-45a/subchapter-h/)

**Indigency, community service and waiver — arts. 45A.252, 45A.254, 45A.257 (former 45.041(a-1), 45.049, 45.0491)**
- [V] **45A.252** requires the court to inquire into the defendant's ability to pay at the plea.
- [V] **45A.254** allows community service to discharge fines or costs for a defendant who lacks sufficient resources or income. Generally no more than **16 hours per week** may be required unless more would not cause undue hardship. **Each 8 hours** of service is credited at **not less than $100**. The defendant may pay off the balance at any time.
- [V] **45A.257(a)**: a fine may be waived on a finding of indigence plus a finding that alternatives such as community service would impose undue hardship. **45A.257(c)**: court costs may be waived on indigence alone, or if the defendant was a child at the time of the offense.
- Sources: [TMCEC blog, "Protections Regarding Fines and Fees in Chapter 45A" (Jan. 27, 2025)](https://tmcecblog.com/2025/01/27/protections-regarding-fines-and-fees-in-chapter-45a-safeguarding-justice-for-all/); [texas.public.law, 45A.254](https://texas.public.law/statutes/tex._code_of_crim._proc._article_45a.254)

### Inferences
- **Scooter offense and DSC (Rating: subject to interpretation, leaning "not eligible").** DSC dismissal requires an offense that "involves the operation of a motor vehicle" (former 45.0511(a), presumably carried into Subch. H). A motor-assisted scooter is very likely not a "motor vehicle" for this purpose (see the Transp. Code §551.352 item under Gaps). So DSC dismissal is probably unavailable as of right. A judge can still order a driving-safety or other course **as a deferred-disposition condition** under 45A.303 ("or another course as directed by the judge"). Deferred disposition (45A.302) is the main dismissal-type option for any fine-only scooter or ordinance charge.
- **Applicability (Rating: clearly established in principle).** 45A.003 says other general Code provisions apply where Chapter 45A is silent. General CCP tools therefore apply in municipal court: art. 39.14 discovery, ch. 24 subpoenas, art. 28.01 pretrial hearings, and arts. 27.08/27.09 exceptions to the substance or form of the complaint.
- **Complaint defects (Rating: clearly established that a defective complaint is challengeable; timing rule unverified).** A defendant who pleads not guilty can check the sworn complaint against 45A.101's list. Points to check:
  - territorial-limits allegation
  - offense date
  - affiant signature
  - "peace and dignity" conclusion
  - opening "In the name and by the authority of the State of Texas"
  - for an ordinance charge, identification of the ordinance violated

  The objection should be raised in writing **before trial**, because former 45.019(f) made defects not objected to before trial waived (see Gaps).
- **Old-number pitfalls.** Many city websites and court forms still cite former Chapter 45 numbers (45.037, 45.0425, 45.051). A guide should give both numbers and tell readers that pre-2025 city pages may be out of date.

## 2. Discovery and public-records routes: art. 39.14 (Michael Morton Act), PIA ch. 552, body-worn-camera statute, subpoenas

### Takeaway
Chapter 45A has no discovery article that I could confirm. 45A.003 imports the Code's general provisions where 45A is silent, so **art. 39.14 discovery applies in municipal court**. This is TMCEC's long-standing view (Rating: **clearly established** that 39.14 governs, with the pro-se limits in 39.14(d) unverified). Offense reports, officer notes and video should be requested from the **prosecutor (city attorney) in writing** under art. 39.14. A parallel Public Information Act request to the **police department** is possible but slower and often blocked by the §552.108 law-enforcement exception while the case is pending. Nothing found suggests a PIA request tolls or extends any court deadline (Rating: **clearly established by absence; no contrary authority found**).

### Cited Findings
- [V] 45A.003: where Chapter 45A has no rule, the judge applies the Code's other general provisions — [Justia, Subch. A](https://law.justia.com/codes/texas/code-of-criminal-procedure/title-1/chapter-45a/subchapter-a/)
- [V] 45A.004: the district-court evidence rules apply in municipal court. Authentication, hearsay and confrontation objections (for example to an officer's report offered instead of live testimony) are available — [Justia, Subch. A](https://law.justia.com/codes/texas/code-of-criminal-procedure/title-1/chapter-45a/subchapter-a/)
- No search result surfaced any Chapter 45A article on discovery. That is consistent with there being none, but the search was not exhaustive ([Justia ch. 45A index pages](https://law.justia.com/codes/texas/code-of-criminal-procedure/title-1/chapter-45a/subchapter-a/)).

### Inferences
- Practical sequence: (1) plead not guilty and request a jury or bench trial; (2) promptly serve a **written art. 39.14 request on the prosecutor**, naming the offense report, officer notes or citation-back notes, body-worn and dash/in-car video, photos, radar or other device data if relevant, and any witness statements; (3) optionally, send a PIA request to the police department as a backup; (4) if the State has not produced, raise it at the pretrial hearing (art. 28.01) and ask for a continuance or exclusion.
- A PIA request runs on its own clock (10 business days for the agency to seek an Attorney General ruling, then up to about 45 business days for the AG). Any trial setting is likely to come first. Treat the PIA route as supplementary, not a substitute for 39.14. Rating: **subject to interpretation** as to practical value.
- **Subpoenas:** municipal courts issue witness subpoenas under CCP ch. 24, via 45A.003. A defendant who wants the citing officer or a civilian witness present should apply for a subpoena well before trial. Rating: clearly established in principle; local procedure unverified.

## 3. Proving the ordinance in effect on the offense date: prior versions, judicial notice, publication, burden

### Takeaway
For an ordinance-only charge, the complaint must identify an offense under a city ordinance committed within city limits. It may conclude "Contrary to the said ordinance" ([V], 45A.101). The State must prove every element beyond a reasonable doubt, which includes an ordinance **in force on the offense date**. Courts normally satisfy this through **judicial notice under Texas Rule of Evidence 204**. The defense's practical job is to obtain the version of the ordinance (and its amendment history) in effect on the offense date, and to object if the complaint cites a provision that did not exist or did not prohibit the conduct on that date (Rating: **clearly established** in principle; specific Rule 204 and Local Gov't Code wording **unverified**).

### Cited Findings
- [V] Ordinance-only complaints "may also conclude with the words 'Contrary to the said ordinance.'" The municipal complaint must allege commission within the city's territorial limits — [FindLaw, art. 45A.101](https://codes.findlaw.com/tx/code-of-criminal-procedure/crim-ptx-crim-pro-art-45a-101/)
- [V] TMCEC: ordinance complaints also must begin "In the name and by the authority of the State of Texas" — [TMCEC Level I 2024–25, ch. 4](https://www.tmcec.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/11/Chapter-4-Charging-and-Pre-trial-2024.pdf)

### Inferences
Steps to get the prior version (Rating: practical guidance; each host and office **unverified**):
1. Read the section's **history note** in the online code ("Ord. No. ____, passed/eff. ____"). It lists each amending ordinance.
2. Pull those ordinance numbers from the **City Secretary** (Dallas City Secretary's Office; the Town Secretary of Highland Park; the City Secretary of University Park). Ask for **certified copies** of the ordinance and any later amendment in effect on the offense date.
3. If the online code host offers archived editions, compare the edition current on the offense date.
4. Request judicial notice in writing, or object, under TRE 204.

Other points:
- If the citation charges a state statute (e.g., Transp. Code ch. 551) instead of an ordinance, the ordinance-history work is unnecessary. The question becomes the statute's text on the offense date, checked through the session-law history notes on statutes.capitol.texas.gov.
- On a **de novo appeal** to county court, the county court must also be supplied with or take notice of the ordinance. Bringing certified copies avoids a gap. Rating: subject to interpretation.

## 4. Appeals: non-record courts (trial de novo), courts of record (on the record), status of Dallas, Highland Park and University Park, and further-appeal limits

### Takeaway
- **Municipal court NOT of record** (Rating: **clearly established**):
  - A motion for new trial must be filed **within 5 days** after judgment and sentence (45A.201).
  - Appeal is to the **county court** for **trial de novo** (45A.202).
  - The appeal is perfected by filing an **appeal bond within 10 days after the judgment is entered** (45A.203). The bond is at least the greater of $50 or (per former 45.0425) **two times the fine and costs**. A late bond leaves the county court without jurisdiction.
- **Municipal court OF record** (Gov't Code ch. 30) (Rating: **clearly established that de novo is barred** [V]; ch. 30 deadline details only partly verified):
  - Appeal is **on the record, not de novo** (45A.202(b) [V]).
  - It requires a **written motion for new trial within 10 days** setting out points of error, and an appeal bond (secondary source: double fine and costs or $100, whichever is greater).
- **Whether Dallas, Highland Park and University Park courts are courts of record: NOT VERIFIED** (Rating: **ambiguous / gap**). This is the single most important open item for the guide.
- Further appeal to the court of appeals is barred where the fine affirmed is $100 or less, unless the sole issue is the constitutionality of the statute or ordinance (art. 4.03, **unverified wording**; Rating: clearly established in substance).

### Cited Findings
**Non-record courts**
- [V] **45A.201 (New Trial):**
  - "a motion for a new trial must be made not later than the fifth day after the imposition of judgment and sentence."
  - The judge may grant a new trial for good cause through the 10th day after judgment ("if the justice or judge considers that justice has not been done the defendant in the trial of the case").
  - (c) If not granted before the 11th day after judgment, the motion is considered denied.
  - (e)–(f) Only one new trial is allowed per case, and the State is not entitled to one.
  - Several city web pages still cite former art. 45.037 with a **one-day** deadline (e.g., Mansfield). Those are outdated.
  - Sources: [FindLaw, 45A.201](https://codes.findlaw.com/tx/code-of-criminal-procedure/crim-ptx-crim-pro-art-45a-201/); [Justia, 45A.201](https://law.justia.com/codes/texas/2023/code-of-criminal-procedure/title-1/chapter-45a/subchapter-e/article-45a-201/); outdated example: [Mansfield "Motion for New Trial/Appeal"](https://www.mansfieldtexas.gov/2069/Motion-for-New-TrialAppeal)
- [V] **45A.202 (Appeal):**
  - Appeals from justice and municipal courts go to the county court.
  - "a de novo trial shall be held on appeal unless the appeal is taken from a municipal court of record and based on error reflected in the record."
  - An appeal may not be dismissed for certain reasons, such as the defendant's failure to give notice of appeal.
  - Source: [Texas Code of Criminal Procedure ch. 45A (texas.public.law)](https://texas.public.law/statutes/tex._code_of_crim._proc._title_1_chapter_45a)
- [V] **45A.203 (Appeal Bond):**
  - The appeal is perfected when the appeal bond is filed with the justice or judge who tried the case "not later than the 10th day after the date the judgment was entered."
  - (b) If the bond is not timely filed, the appellate court lacks jurisdiction and must remand the case for execution of the sentence.
  - The minimum bond is the greater of two amounts, one of which is **$50**. The other prong was cut off in the search excerpt. Former art. 45.0425 set it at "two times the amount of the fine and costs adjudged against the defendant" and "may not in any case be for an amount less than $50."
  - If the bond otherwise meets the Code's requirements, the court "shall approve the appeal bond" without requiring an appearance, at the amount previously communicated under art. 27.14(b).
  - Sources: [texas.public.law, 45A.203](https://texas.public.law/statutes/tex._code_of_crim._proc._article_45a.203); [texas.public.law, former 45.0425](https://texas.public.law/statutes/tex._code_of_crim._proc._article_45.0425); [Onecle, former 45.0425](https://law.onecle.com/texas/criminal-procedure/45.0425.html)

**Courts of record**
- [V] Secondary source (a city guide, not statute): for a municipal **court of record** appeal, the bond is "double the amount of the fine and costs assessed against you or $100, whichever amount is greater," and the motion for new trial must be filed within 10 days — [North Richland Hills, "Appeal from a Municipal Court of Record"](https://www.nrhtx.com/DocumentCenter/View/620). Other city appeal guides: [Farmersville](https://www.farmersvilletx.com/media/711); [Austin "A Citizen's Guide to Appeal" (rev. 12/31/2019)](https://www.austintexas.gov/sites/default/files/files/Municipal_Court/Appeal_Instructions.pdf)
- [V] A 1999 bill analysis (H.B. 651, 76th Leg.) shows the Legislature prohibiting trial de novo for appeals from a municipal court of record and setting the bond at "$100 or twice the fines and costs" — [HBA H.B. 651 (76R)](https://capitol.texas.gov/tlodocs/76R/analysis/html/HB00651I.htm)

### Inferences
- **Deadline interplay in a non-record court:**
  - day 5: last day to file a motion for new trial
  - day 10: last day the judge may grant it
  - day 10: last day to file the appeal bond, counted from judgment entry, not from denial of the motion
  - day 11: a pending motion is deemed denied

  Because the bond is due on day 10 regardless, a defendant should **not wait** for a ruling on the motion before posting the appeal bond. Rating: **clearly established** on the verified text. Confirm whether 45A.203 measures from "judgment entered" in all cases.
- **Practical effect of de novo appeal:** a fresh trial in county court (the Dallas County Criminal Courts at Law), prosecuted by the county (Dallas County District Attorney's Office), unless the county attorney consents to the city attorney handling it (45A.005 [V]).
- **Court-of-record appeal:** no new evidence. The defendant must (a) **ask for a record (court reporter or recording) at trial**, (b) file a timely motion for new trial listing points of error, (c) post the bond, and (d) file a brief. Missing the trial record is fatal to most appellate issues. Rating: clearly established in principle; specific Gov't Code §§ unverified.
- **Determining the status of each court:**
  1. Look at the judgment or citation paperwork ("Municipal Court of Record No. __").
  2. Check the city ordinance creating the court (Gov't Code §30.00003 allows creation by ordinance).
  3. Check the Texas Judicial Branch municipal-court directory.
  4. Ask the clerk before trial.

## 6. Typical penalties, court costs, and DPS driving-record reporting for a scooter offense

### Takeaway
