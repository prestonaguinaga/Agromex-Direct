## Q1. Can a stand-up scooter be a Texas "electric bicycle" (Ch. 664 / §541.201)? What about seated "e-scooters" with pedals?

### Takeaway
No. A pedal-less stand-up scooter cannot be a Texas electric bicycle. Since HB 2188 (2019), an e-bike must first be a "bicycle" and must have **fully operable pedals**, a motor of **fewer than 750 W**, and a **top assisted speed of 28 mph or less**, in one of three classes. E-bikes made or sold on or after 1/1/2020 also need a manufacturer class label. A seated, pedal-equipped "e-scooter" or moped-style e-bike *can* qualify if it meets every element, including the label and the sub-750 W motor. **[Clear]** for the pedal-less scooter. **[Interpretive]** for retrofitted pedals.

### Cited Findings

### Inferences
- **Pedal-less stand-up scooter → not an e-bike. [Clear]** Element (A)(i), "fully operable pedals," is missing, and so is the human-power "bicycle" predicate. The classification fails at the definitional threshold.
- **Motor ≥750 W → never an e-bike, even with pedals. [Clear]** The statute says "fewer than 750 watts." A 750 W motor fails, as does a 1000 W one. Rated versus peak wattage is not addressed in the extracts I saw. **[Ambiguous]**
- **Speed:** A device whose motor keeps assisting above 28 mph is outside the definition. A throttle-only device (no pedaling) fits only Class 2, which tops out at 20 mph. So a throttle scooter with pedals bolted on and a 25–40 mph throttle top speed fails the class structure. **[Clear on text]**
- **Retrofitting pedals onto a standing-deck scooter. [Interpretive; practically weak]**
  - "Fully operable" implies pedals that can actually propel the device by human power through a drivetrain. Footpegs or decorative cranks would not do that.
  - The amended "bicycle" definition requires a device capable of being ridden solely by human power.
  - A small-wheel scooter would likely also fail any surviving 14-inch-wheel element (not verified).
  - Separately, §664.002 puts the labeling duty on the "person who manufactures or sells" the e-bike. A home-built conversion of a post-2020 device would have no compliant label, and the label must be replaced if speed or power changes. Converting to e-bike status is therefore not a realistic lawful path for a stand-up scooter.
- **Seated "e-scooters" with real pedals** (e.g., moped-style e-bikes with 14–20 in wheels and a chain drive) can be lawful Class 1/2/3 e-bikes if all of the following hold. **[Clear on text; fact-specific per model]**
  - they have fully operable pedals,
  - the motor is under 750 W,
  - assisted speed is 20 mph (Class 1/2) or 28 mph pedal-assist (Class 3), and
  - they carry the §664.002 label.
- **E-bike status is the most favorable status there is.** The vehicle is not a "motor vehicle" (§541.201(11)), so no title, registration, license, or insurance is needed and road access is guaranteed (§551.106). But that status is legitimately available only for a true pedal bicycle that meets the class limits.

## Q2. Moped / "motor-driven cycle" / motorcycle — definitions, and the full paperwork path (title, registration, plates, inspection, license, insurance, helmet, FMVSS)

### Takeaway
After HB 3171 (2019), Texas has only **mopeds** and **motorcycles**. "Motor-driven cycle" was repealed. Both are **motor vehicles "equipped with a rider's saddle and designed to have when propelled not more than three wheels on the ground."** A standing-deck scooter has no rider's saddle, so it is neither one. TxDMV and the AG have treated the saddle as dispositive for saddle-less electric three-wheelers. A seated, highway-capable two-wheeler is a moped (≤30 mph, ≤5 bhp) or otherwise a motorcycle. Either way it must be titled, registered at $30/yr, and insured. Licensing is **Class M** (moped licensing is ambiguous after 2019), and riders under 21 must wear helmets. Titling in practice requires an MCO from a manufacturer and FMVSS compliance, which a no-VIN, no-label e-scooter lacks. **[Clear]** on definitions. **[Ambiguous]** on moped licensing and helmet details.

### Inferences
- **Stand-up scooter, no seat → not a moped and not a motorcycle. [Clear on text; strengthened by TxDMV/AG practice]** Both definitions require "equipped with a rider's saddle." TxDMV has applied the saddle element literally to deny motorcycle classification (KP-0331 request).
- **Adding a seat kit → could arguably supply a "rider's saddle," but the device still has to be a "motor vehicle" first. [Interpretive]** Ch. 502 defines a motor vehicle simply as "a vehicle that is self-propelled" (see Gaps). The scooter-specific carve-out in §551.352(e) (Q6) is what keeps a qualifying motor-assisted scooter outside motor-vehicle rules. The MAS definition expressly contemplates a deck on which a person may "stand or sit," so a seat alone should not destroy MAS status. Q6 covers what can.
- **If a seated scooter is a moped or motorcycle, the full path is:**
  1. **Title:** MCO from the manufacturer, or a prior title, filed with the county tax assessor-collector.
  2. **Registration:** $30/yr plus fees, plus the Inspection Program Replacement Fee. Plate.
  3. **Safety inspection:** none since 1/1/2025 for non-commercial vehicles (HB 3297).
  4. **Insurance:** §601.051 liability coverage.
  5. **License:** Class M (age 15 with the 250cc "I" restriction; moped treatment ambiguous).
  6. **Helmet:** under 21.
  7. **Equipment:** Ch. 547 lighting, mirror, horn, brakes (see Gaps).
  8. **Federal:** a manufacturer-certified FMVSS vehicle with a 17-character VIN.
  
  For a no-VIN, no-label, no-MCO e-scooter, **step 1 cannot be completed**. Without an MCO there is no title, and without a title there is no registration. **[Clear in practice; see Q3]**
- **A 15–40 mph seated scooter that falls out of MAS and cannot be titled lands in an unregistrable category** on public roads. Exposure: no registration (Ch. 502), no license (Ch. 521), no financial responsibility (§601.051), and, if under 21, no helmet. **[Interpretive — depends on whether MAS status is lost; see Q6]**
- **Speed ≤30 mph plus a saddle points to "moped"; above 30 mph, "motorcycle."** The moped cap is "in one mile" (attainable speed). An electronically limited 30 mph unit could arguably be a moped if it is otherwise a titled motor vehicle; "cannot attain" leans toward actual capability. **[Interpretive]**
- **Electric motor and the moped power cap:** the five-brake-horsepower cap (≈3.7 kW) is not exceeded by 250–1000 W hub motors. The 50 cc limit applies only to internal combustion. So power does not push a seated e-scooter out of "moped"; speed and saddle do. **[Clear on text]**

## Q3. Can an individual title a homemade/assembled or non-FMVSS (no VIN, no label) scooter? What do federal law and NHTSA say about e-scooters as "motor vehicles"?

### Takeaway
TxDMV's assembled-vehicle route bars vehicles that:
- are "designed for off-highway use … only,"
- merge two or more vehicle classes, or
- use parts not compliant with an FMVSS "if a standard exists."

So an individual realistically **cannot** turn a non-FMVSS consumer e-scooter into a titled road vehicle. **[Clear on TxDMV policy; application to a scooter is inference]**

Federally, NHTSA's long-standing interpretations treat about **20 mph** as the practical line. Small scooters that **cannot reach 20 mph** are generally *not* "motor vehicles," so FMVSS does not apply. Those **≥20 mph with on-road equipment** (lights, mirrors, turn signals) are treated as motor vehicles that the *manufacturer/importer* must certify. FMVSS compliance is a manufacturer or importer obligation. The rider's obligations come from Texas law. **[Clear that this is NHTSA's interpretive position; letters are non-binding]**

## Q4. EPAMD (§551.201–.203), low-speed/neighborhood electric vehicles, golf carts and off-highway vehicles — can a scooter qualify?

### Takeaway
No. An EPAMD must have **two non-tandem (side-by-side) wheels and be self-balancing** (Segway-type). A conventional inline-wheel scooter fails both elements. **[Clear]** NEV/LSV and off-highway categories are four-wheel or off-road classes and are irrelevant to a two-wheel stand-up scooter. **[Clear in substance; specific text partly unverified]**

## Q5. Do modifications change the classification? (speed-limiting, removing or adding a seat, adding pedals, motor swap) — "designed to" versus actual capability; authority on reclassification by modification

### Takeaway
Texas definitions mix **design-based** elements with **capability-based** elements.
- Design-based: "designed to have … not more than three wheels," the MAS "deck designed to allow a person to stand or sit," and "designed for use on a highway" in §601.002.
- Capability-based: the moped's "cannot attain a speed in one mile of more than 30 mph," e-bike "fully operable pedals," "fewer than 750 watts," and "top assisted speed."

**Real, functional changes can change classification:** pedals plus a drivetrain, a motor change, a seat/saddle, or a hard speed cap. **Cosmetic or easily reversible changes are risky** and may be disregarded. I found **no Texas appellate case or AG opinion** squarely addressing reclassification of a scooter by modification. KP-0331 shows TxDMV and the AG look at actual features (presence of a saddle), and §664.002 signals that the Legislature expects modified e-bikes to be re-labeled to match actual speed and power. **[Interpretive]**

### Cited Findings
- **§664.002:** if a modification changes speed or power so that the e-bike label is no longer accurate, the label must be replaced (paraphrase). The statute contemplates that post-sale modifications change the operative facts. — [Ch. 664](https://tcss.legis.texas.gov/resources/TN/htm/TN.664.htm)
- **KP-0331 (2020):** TxDMV treated the absence of a rider's "saddle" as dispositive against motorcycle status for three-wheeled electric low-speed vehicles. The AG said vehicle-specific classification "would depend on particular facts, which cannot be resolved in the opinion process." — [KP-0331](https://www.texasattorneygeneral.gov/sites/default/files/opinion-files/opinion/2020/kp-0331.pdf)
- **43 TAC §217.3(1)(B) (2020):** moped-title determinations are made by applying the §541.201 definition, with no longer any DPS list. TxDMV therefore decides moped status case by case from vehicle facts. — [TxDMV 217.3 adoption](https://ftp.txdmv.gov/pub/txdmv-info/ogc/rules/2020/OGC_217.3_Mopeds-Adopt.pdf)
- **TxDMV will not title** a vehicle "constructed by merging two or more vehicle classes." — [TxDMV, Assembled Vehicles](https://www.txdmv.gov/motorists/assembled-vehicles)
- **NHTSA uses actual top speed and configuration** (seatless, folding steering, ≤20 mph) as indicators that a device is not "manufactured primarily for use on public streets." — [NHTSA Interp. 08-002289as](https://www.nhtsa.gov/interpretations/08-002289as); [NHTSA Interp. nht95-1.6](https://www.nhtsa.gov/interpretations/nht95-16)

Several defense-firm blogs report none exists. — [Chambers Law, Austin (secondary)](https://chambers-law.com/austinscooter/); [DWI Lawyer Houston (secondary)](https://www.dwilawyerhouston.com/blog/bicycles-scooters-and-dwis/)

## Q6. Practical bottom line: is "motor-assisted scooter" the most favorable classification, and which features push a scooter out of it into an unregistrable motor-vehicle category?

### Takeaway
Yes. For a stand-up e-scooter, **"motor-assisted scooter" (MAS, §551.351) is the classification that is both available and most favorable.** §551.352(e) says "A provision of this title applicable to a motor vehicle does not apply to a motor-assisted scooter." That removes Title 7's title, registration, license, insurance, inspection and helmet provisions. MAS operation is generally governed by bicycle rules, on roads posted ≤35 mph, bike paths and sidewalks, subject to local prohibition.

The risks of falling **out** of MAS are:
- **(a) the "gas or electric motor not exceeding 40 cubic centimeters" element**, which is meaningless for electric motors and so ambiguous for high-power units;
- **(b) no ability "to be propelled by human power alone"**;
- **(c) the "pocket bike or minimotorbike" exclusion**, which a seat or saddle may trigger.

A seated, high-speed, high-power device that is not a MAS and cannot be titled creates exposure to unregistered-vehicle, no-license and no-insurance citations. **Separately, Penal Code DWI applies regardless.** **[MAS exemption Clear; edge cases Ambiguous]**

### Cited Findings
- **§551.351 MAS definition (search extract of 2024 statute; elements confirmed, partly paraphrased):** a self-propelled device with:
  - (A) at least two wheels in contact with the ground during operation;
  - (B) a braking system capable of stopping the device under typical operating conditions;
  - (C) "a gas or electric motor not exceeding 40 cubic centimeters";
  - (D) "a deck designed to allow a person to stand or sit while operating the device"; and
  - (E) the ability to be propelled by human power alone.
  
  The term does not include a "pocket bike or minimotorbike," which the code separately defines by an engine displacement under 50 cc. — [Justia §551.351 (2024)](https://law.justia.com/codes/texas/2024/transportation-code/title-7/subtitle-c/chapter-551/subchapter-e/section-551-351/); [FindLaw §551.351](https://codes.findlaw.com/tx/transportation-code/transp-sect-551-351/); [Texas Public Law §551.351](https://texas.public.law/statutes/tex._transp._code_section_551.351)

Treat conversions as unofficial. — [LegalClarity (secondary)](https://legalclarity.org/are-scooters-street-legal-in-texas/); [Levy Electric (secondary)](https://www.levyelectric.com/resources/electric-scooters-and-licensing-requirements-in-texas)

**Features that can push a scooter OUT of MAS** (and toward the unregistrable motor-vehicle bucket):
1. **No human-power capability.** The MAS element is "ability to be propelled by human power alone." A device that cannot be kicked along (e.g., direct-drive hub drag, heavy seated designs, no usable deck for kicking) is arguably outside MAS. **[Interpretive]**
2. **High-power motors versus "40 cc."** An electric motor has no displacement. Two readings:
   - Literal reading: any electric motor satisfies "not exceeding 40 cc," since it has 0 cc. That is favorable.
   - Purposive reading: 40 cc is a power proxy (~1–2 hp is a common folk conversion, but unofficial), so a 1000+ W, 30–40 mph unit exceeds it.
   
   No AG opinion or case resolving this was found. The failed SB 549 shows the Legislature considered, and did not enact, a 2,000 W electric cap. **[Ambiguous]**
3. **Seat/saddle + not designed for highway + <50 cc → "pocket bike/minimotorbike" exclusion.** The full definition was not retrieved (see Gaps). Adding a seat is the feature most likely to move a scooter from MAS toward this excluded and more restricted category. Combined with a "rider's saddle," it also supplies the predicate for moped/motorcycle status, which the device cannot satisfy for title. **[Interpretive]**
4. **Speed.** The retrieved MAS definition has no speed cap, so a 40 mph stand-up scooter is not excluded from MAS by speed alone under the text. **[Clear on text as retrieved]** Speed matters federally (≥20 mph), for the moped (≤30) versus motorcycle split if a saddle exists, and practically for police discretion. MAS operation is also limited to roads posted ≤35 mph (secondary summary of §551.352(a); exact text not retrieved).

**Exposure if a device is NOT a MAS but is used on public roads as a self-propelled vehicle:** no registration (Ch. 502), no driver license (Ch. 521), no financial responsibility (§601.051), possibly no helmet if under 21 and treated as a motorcycle, and equipment violations (Ch. 547). It cannot be cured, because there is no title path for a non-FMVSS, no-MCO device. **[Interpretive — depends on a fact-finder deciding MAS status is lost]**

**Recommendation framing for the report (lawful options only):**
- (1) Keep a stand-up scooter squarely within MAS: stand-up deck, working brake, kickable, no saddle, moderate power and speed. Comply with local rules.
- (2) If a seated, pedal-capable ride is wanted, buy a **properly labeled Class 1/2/3 e-bike** (<750 W, fully operable pedals).
- (3) If a seated, faster vehicle is wanted, buy a **factory FMVSS-certified electric moped or motorcycle** with a VIN and MCO, then title, register, insure, get a Class M license, and wear a helmet if under 21.

Do **not** add seats, decals, or cosmetic pedals to try to borrow another class. Those changes either do nothing legally or move the device into an unregistrable category.
